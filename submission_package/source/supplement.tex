\section{Purpose, scope, and evidence hierarchy}

This supplement documents the estimands, diagnostic summaries, sensitivity
analyses, and artifact contract supporting the main article. It does not add a
new population-level claim. The evidence hierarchy used throughout the paper is:
(i) the 144-cell four-dataset YOLO landscape for exploratory factorial
characterization; (ii) the selection-disjoint VOC/KITTI final holdout as the strongest
selection-disjoint conditional evidence; (iii) targeted realization, training,
calibration, class-universe, codec, weighting, and runtime sensitivities; and
(iv) RT-DETR and RetinaNet experiments as recipe-portability stress cases;
(v) a three-block compound quantizer-attachment comparison and a one-block
policy-matched rebuild with paired image uncertainty;
and (vi) descriptive, non-additive TIDE error diagnostics on the primary grid.
The last two layers target recipe contrasts and error burdens, respectively,
not additional estimates of the primary four-dataset interaction.

The labels \emph{INT8} and \emph{FP8} denote the complete recorded TensorRT
treatments---quantizer placement, Q/DQ coverage, calibration or scale
construction, graph rewriting, mixed-precision fallbacks, engine building, and
frozen decoding---rather than datatype effects in isolation. All quantities are
reported in AP points unless stated otherwise.

\section{Atomic paired design and estimands}

For a fixed dataset $d$, checkpoint $m$, endpoint $b$, and corruption condition
$c$, let $\Jclean$ denote the exploratory deterministic JPEG-quality-95 matched-clean control. The explicit $\Jclean$ label avoids confusion with AP at IoU 0.95. For the final holdout, replace $\Jclean$ throughout the equations by original-source clean; that evidence layer is not terminal-codec matched. Retention measures likewise use each layer's recorded clean definition.
For quantized treatment $q$, define the FP32-referenced quantization gap and the
clean-adjusted excess gap as
\begin{align}
Q_{d,m,q,b,c}
 &= \AP_{d,m,\mathrm{FP32},b,c}-\AP_{d,m,q,b,c},\\
E_{d,m,q,b,c}
 &= Q_{d,m,q,b,c}-Q_{d,m,q,b,\Jclean}.
\end{align}
A positive $E$ means that corruption widens the treatment--FP32 discrepancy
relative to matched clean; a negative $E$ means that the discrepancy contracts.
Neither sign is, by itself, evidence of retained absolute accuracy.

The direct treatment interaction is computed inside each common-image resample:
\begin{align}
\DeltaE_{d,m,b,c}
 &= E_{d,m,\mathrm{INT8},b,c}-E_{d,m,\mathrm{FP8},b,c}\\
 &= \bigl(\AP_{\mathrm{FP8},c}-\AP_{\mathrm{INT8},c}\bigr)
  -\bigl(\AP_{\mathrm{FP8},\Jclean}-\AP_{\mathrm{INT8},\Jclean}\bigr).
\end{align}
The FP32 term cancels algebraically. Consequently, $\DeltaE$ asks whether the
FP8--INT8 discrepancy changes under corruption after removing the discrepancy
already present on matched clean inputs. A positive value indicates an increase
in the signed gap; a negative value indicates a decrease. ``Expansion'' and
``contraction'' do not necessarily describe the absolute gap magnitude if its
sign changes. This is a descriptive two-by-two
interaction contrast, not a causal difference-in-differences estimator.

For area endpoints, the within-treatment size contrast is
\begin{equation}
\Psi_{d,m,q,c}=E_{d,m,q,S,c}-E_{d,m,q,L,c},
\end{equation}
with direct size interaction $\DeltaPsi=\Psi_{\mathrm{INT8}}-
\Psi_{\mathrm{FP8}}$. TT100K uses its native height-based small-like and
large-like endpoints, which are reported separately and never pooled
numerically with the COCO/VOC/KITTI area endpoints.

The signed clean-to-corrupted AP loss used by the absolute-accuracy guardrail is
\begin{equation}
D_{d,m,p,b,c}=\AP_{d,m,p,b,\Jclean}-\AP_{d,m,p,b,c}.
\end{equation}
Thus $D>0$ is an AP loss and $D<0$ is an AP gain; despite its role in the
absolute-accuracy guardrail, $D$ is not an absolute value.
The direct interaction can therefore also be written as
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{d,m,b,c}=D_{d,m,\mathrm{INT8},b,c}-D_{d,m,\mathrm{FP8},b,c}.
\end{equation}
The protocol therefore uses an \emph{absolute-accuracy guardrail}: $D$, corrupted AP, and low-AP
inventories are reported beside relative interactions. This is a reporting requirement, not a formal
statistical correction for censoring, an AP floor, or a deployment threshold.

As a secondary scale sensitivity, relative retention normalizes each corrupted
arm by that treatment's own matched-clean AP:
\begin{align}
R_{d,m,q,b,c}
 &=100\frac{\AP_{d,m,q,b,c}}{\AP_{d,m,q,b,\Jclean}},\\
\DeltaR_{d,m,b,c}
 &=R_{d,m,\mathrm{FP8},b,c}-R_{d,m,\mathrm{INT8},b,c}.
\end{align}
$\DeltaR$ is reported in percentage points (pp); positive values mean that FP8
retains a larger percentage of its own matched-clean AP. Because this ratio
changes the scale and effective weighting induced by clean AP, it is a
sensitivity estimand rather than a replacement for the AP-point interaction
$\DeltaE$.

The image is the resampling unit. Each direct comparison reuses one deterministic
schedule of 2,000 image-index bootstrap draws across all four INT8/FP8 by
clean/corrupted arms. Percentile intervals quantify finite-evaluation-set
uncertainty conditional on the frozen datasets, checkpoints, calibration lists,
engines, corruption materializations, and decoder. They do not propagate a
population distribution over datasets, training runs, calibration runs, or
corruption generators.

\section{Point-sign interpretation diagnostic}

The empirical consequence of clean adjustment can be quantified. For each
of the 144 exploratory cells, the raw corrupted gap satisfies
\begin{equation}
(\AP_{\mathrm{FP8}}-\AP_{\mathrm{INT8}})_{c}
=(\AP_{\mathrm{FP8}}-\AP_{\mathrm{INT8}})_{\Jclean}+\DeltaE.
\end{equation}
The raw corrupted comparison favored FP8 in 134 cells, but 56 of those cells
had negative $\DeltaE$ point estimates after subtracting the matched-clean
discrepancy (Table~\ref{tab:decision-impact}). Of these 56 point-sign
disagreements, 17 paired percentile intervals lay entirely below zero and 39 crossed
zero; 56 is therefore not a count of statistically resolved discoveries.
Point-estimate sign disagreements occur in 38.9\% of the finite grid. The algebraic
mixing of clean fidelity and corruption-associated interaction applies to
every raw corrupted gap, including cells without a sign disagreement.

Missing fields are not reconstructed from current defaults. This updates the holdout status, without retroactively inserting current settings into historical records. \section{Selection-disjoint holdout and corruption-realization sensitivities}
\label{sec:holdout-evidence}

\paragraph{Prediction-level worked example.}
The separate V4 holdout example contains full predictions and converted annotations for KITTI final/YOLO11m/fog severity 1, including original-source clean inputs. Its fresh-extraction test uses NumPy 2.4.4 and pycocotools 2.0.11, and regenerates the historical 2,000-draw schedule using the recorded seed 852756759 and the retained sampler. Predictions and annotations, not precomputed AP arrays, supply the evaluation. The four AP values in the main text and the paired interval are checked against independently retained metric and bootstrap summaries. Raw historical run records are unchanged. Explicit settings sidecars bind each original command and the corresponding runner/preprocessing/decoder source hashes; the evaluator used for this recomputation is pinned separately. This supports a tested prediction-level reconstruction of one specified holdout condition, not reproduction of every experiment or validation of population-level interval coverage.

The selection-disjoint branch froze disjoint checkpoint-selection and final image lists
before training. VOC used 8,218 training, 2,510 selection, and 5,823 final
images. For KITTI, the 1,496-image selection partition was reserved first; a
deterministic class-covered 20\% subset of the remaining 5,985 images formed
the 1,197-image final set, and the other 4,788 images formed the training set
(Table~\ref{tab:holdout-splits}). Calibration identities were drawn only
from training data. The final factorial rerun retained 72 equal-weight
VOC/KITTI cells and reused a common 2,000-draw schedule within each dataset.

The six-block four-AP table in the main paper uses unrounded retained metrics.
The mean corrupted FP8--INT8 gap is $+1.050221$ AP with paired percentiles
$[+0.910530,+1.043533,+1.155391]$. Its interval is recovered by recomputing
twelve original-clean AP vectors on the historical dataset-specific schedules
and adding their paired FP8--INT8 differences to the retained within-draw
interaction vectors. The sampler source hash, seed, image order, clean
prediction/input bindings and shared FP32 references are checked before this
combination; uniform-sample AP reconstructs the historical clean metrics.
Historical NumPy/evaluator version fields were not recorded, so the recovery
registry distinguishes reconstructed schedules and the pinned current
evaluation from a complete historical-environment attestation. No corrupted
inference is rerun, and no interval endpoints are added.

Its balanced direct interaction was -0.55 AP points with percentile interval
$[-0.78,-0.29]$; VOC was -0.43 $[-0.54,-0.29]$ and KITTI was -0.67
$[-1.13,-0.20]$. These holdout contrasts use original-source clean inputs, not
the exploratory terminal JPEG-95 control. FP8 retained 1.60 more original-clean AP on average, and the
clean advantage was positive in all six dataset--capacity blocks. Nine of 144
holdout treatment arms were below 5 AP and 18 were below 10 AP. This branch
removes evaluation-after-selection reuse for these two datasets, but it does
not convert the broader exploratory grid into a prospective confirmatory study.

\section{Quantizer-attachment sensitivity: paired execution and uncertainty}
\label{sec:supp-attachment}

The attachment experiment covers VOC--YOLO11m, VOC--RT-DETR-L, and KITTI--RetinaNet, each with four corruptions and three severities. It compares the retained default INT8 treatment with a newly built INT8 graph aligned to the frozen FP8 source-compute input contract. The original FP8 engine, predictions, and metrics are reused in both recipe contrasts. This construction controls the specified attachment contract rather than all properties of quantization or execution. Checkpoints and calibration lists remain fixed within each block. There are 39 new INT8 metric arms and 39 reused FP8 arms, yielding 36 direct cells. These blocks use the historical VOC and KITTI image universes, not the separate final holdouts, and were selected for a targeted recipe diagnostic rather than representative architecture sampling.

The attachment contract is anchored to named Conv, Gemm, MatMul, and Add operators in the frozen source graph. For each source input position, it records whether the FP8 graph supplies that input through Q/DQ or leaves it unquantized. In particular, partially quantized Add inputs are not treated as fully quantized operators. Candidate INT8 Q/DQ attachments that are absent from this contract are bypassed only when their raw input resolves to the frozen source edge; missing required attachments or altered source anchors fail validation. Output attachments are checked as well. Orphan Q/DQ nodes are removed after bypassing, and the normalized attachment contract must match the FP8 baseline exactly. Quantizer names, scales, zero-point datatypes, and graph node order are excluded from the matching definition. This is input-policy alignment, not equality of all graph operations, numerical error, or runtime kernels.

In this initial three-block experiment, the aligned INT8 engine validator requires TF32 to be disabled, whereas the retained execution summary records that historical default engines may permit TF32. The summary does not establish actual historical kernel arithmetic. Consequently, attachment changes, engine rebuilding and potentially TF32 policy are combined in the treatment comparison. Holding FP8 fixed cancels its contribution to $\Omega$; it does not cancel an INT8 build-policy difference. These three-block AP gains and intervals remain descriptive comparisons of two recorded compound recipes. The subsequent policy-matched KITTI--RetinaNet follow-up below addresses the specified build-policy difference in one block only.

The endpoint is $\Omega=\Delta E_{\mathrm{default}}-\Delta E_{\mathrm{aligned}}$, computed from COCO-style AP@[0.50:0.95] in AP points. VOC uses 4,952 images and bootstrap seed 4198153701; KITTI uses 1,496 images and seed 653146354. For each dataset, NumPy's generator creates one ordered $2000\times N$ integer-position matrix, with replacement. The same row is used for every associated clean/corrupted, INT8/FP8, and default/aligned term. Both VOC blocks share the VOC schedule. Duplicate positions remain duplicated evaluator entries; stable score sorting preserves their order. Block means are computed within each draw, followed by an equal-block mean and percentile endpoints. The intervals describe image resampling conditional on the selected checkpoints, calibration inputs, builds, and corruption materializations.

The computational accelerator preserves the original area-zero AP accumulation order while omitting unused area endpoints and evaluating identical FP8 inputs only once. It retains stable sorting, ordered repeated positions, float64 arithmetic, and the original contrast subtraction order. On a completed KITTI--RetinaNet fog/severity-1 cell, all 2,000 accelerated values matched the retained default-interaction, aligned-interaction, and $\Omega$ vectors bit for bit; maximum absolute differences were zero and percentile endpoints matched. The 24 previously completed cells were retained unchanged. Accelerator source, parity report, clean draw cache, and generated cell records are hash-bound separately from the frozen original execution package. Concurrent execution time is not admissible latency evidence.

The equal-block estimate is $\Omega=-0.970$, with interval $[-1.202,-0.783]$. It is largely driven by KITTI--RetinaNet: the estimate is $-3.058$, with interval $[-3.698,-2.586]$. For VOC--YOLO11m the estimate is $+0.166$, with interval $[-0.037,+0.371]$. For VOC--RT-DETR-L it is $-0.018$, with interval $[-0.205,+0.229]$. This finite selection cannot separate dataset from architecture, establish an architecture-wide response, or identify a failing layer. A reduced magnitude of negative interaction does not demonstrate recovery of absolute corrupted accuracy.

Table~\ref{tab:attachment-guardrail} supplies the absolute-accuracy check for this same experiment. Because FP8 is reused, $\Omega$ equals the INT8 gain under corruption minus the INT8 gain on matched clean. For KITTI--RetinaNet these gains are 4.640 and 7.698 AP points, respectively; the default recipe's mean corrupted AP is slightly above its matched-clean AP, so its mean signed loss is negative. Alignment improves both accuracies but does not close the large FP8 gap. For VOC--YOLO11m the corrupted gain exceeds the clean gain; its aligned interaction becomes more negative despite improved absolute AP.

\section{Retained-draw audit of clean--corrupted covariance}

This post hoc protocol-component audit uses all 144 primary cells and their 2,000 retained paired image draws. The clean INT8 and FP8 AP caches and direct-interaction caches are checked against their committed hashes, image-schedule identity and clean-source bindings; the stored interaction percentiles are independently reproduced. For each cell and draw $b$, define $g_0^{(b)}=\AP_{\mathrm{FP8},\Jclean}^{(b)}-\AP_{\mathrm{INT8},\Jclean}^{(b)}$ and reconstruct $g_c^{(b)}=g_0^{(b)}+\Delta E^{(b)}$. With sample variances and covariance using divisor $B-1$, the identity is
\[
s^2(\Delta E)=s^2(g_c)+s^2(g_0)-2\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(g_c,g_0).
\]
We compare the paired SD with $\sqrt{s^2(g_c)+s^2(g_0)}$, a zero-covariance counterfactual that preserves each gap's empirical marginal variation. INT8--FP8 pairing within clean and within corrupted conditions remains intact. This calculation does not resample four independent arms, alter point estimates, generate new confidence intervals, or test confidence-interval coverage. No cross-cell independence assumption is used to construct a new pooled interval.

\section{Operational training and materialization details}

\subsection{Training, checkpoint selection, and holdout rerun}


We evaluate YOLO11n, YOLO11m, and YOLO11x. VOC and KITTI use $640\times640$ inputs; TT100K uses $1280\times1280$ so small signs retain more pixels. Every transfer model is initialized from the corresponding official COCO-pretrained Ultralytics checkpoint, with a frozen SHA-256 identity for each capacity. Exploratory training runs for 100 epochs with automatic mixed precision (AMP), seed 20260807, requested deterministic execution, eight workers, zero patience, and automatically selected optimizer and batch. Realized batches are 52/14/6 for VOC, 55/14/6 for KITTI, and 11/3/1 for TT100K (n/m/x). Ultralytics 8.4.95 selects stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGD; learning rate 0.01, momentum 0.9) for VOC, AdamW (learning rate $8.33\times10^{-4}$, betas 0.9/0.999) for KITTI, and AdamW (learning rate $4.4\times10^{-5}$, betas 0.9/0.999) for TT100K. Weight decay is configured as $5\times10^{-4}$ and internally scaled by effective batch and accumulation; realized values and best epochs appear in Table~\ref{tab:training-realizations}.

Augmentation uses hue, saturation, and value amplitudes of 0.015, 0.7, and 0.4, translation 0.1, scale 0.5, horizontal-flip probability 0.5, and mosaic probability 1.0, disabled for the final ten epochs. MixUp, CutMix, copy--paste, rotation, shear, and perspective are zero. The training table reports the maximum native Ultralytics validation mAP$_{50:95}$ in retained training CSVs and the recorded best-checkpoint identity. This is not a claim that native validation and final COCOeval use the same contract or that the historical checkpoint-save implementation has been independently recovered. The held-out rerun retains the same initialization identities, uses 100 epochs, batch 16, four workers, AMP, and seed 20260818; automatic selection gives SGD with momentum for VOC and AdamW for KITTI. COCO uses official checkpoints directly without retraining.

For the final rerun, all data partitions are frozen through a split registry generated with seed 20260818 before retraining. The VOC training set combines 8,218 images from train2007 and train2012. The 2,510 images in val2007 are used for checkpoint selection, whereas the 5,823 images in val2012 form the final evaluation set and are not involved in model selection. For KITTI, 1,496 of the 7,481 labeled images are first retained for selection. A deterministic, label-aware procedure then reserves a class-covering 20\% subset of the remaining 5,985 images (1,197 images) for final evaluation; the other 4,788 images form the training partition. Each final partition contains every observed class. This construction protects class coverage but defines a conditional, designed KITTI holdout rather than a random sample of the official data distribution. Calibration samples are drawn only from the associated training partition. Confidence threshold, NMS, decoder, calibration procedure, and parity gates are frozen before metrics on the final images are calculated. Supplementary material reports image, object, and class counts for all partitions.


\paragraph{Selection-metric boundary.}
TT100K's native validation ledger and final test AP use different image universes and evaluator settings. The retained acquisition record lists 7,641 validation images with 1,097 label files, versus 3,067 final test images with 3,067 label files. A record-level audit against the original annotation JSON verifies that the other 6,544 validation records contain explicit empty object lists. It finds no missing retained positive labels, excluded-category boxes or conversion mismatches. The 6,103 training records contain 16,524 boxes; the positive validation records contain 1,644 boxes; the final test records contain 8,181 boxes. Native arguments record \texttt{max\_det=300}, \texttt{save\_json=false}, and \texttt{cls\_remap=true}; final COCOeval uses \texttt{maxDets}=100. The native n/m/x maxima are 7.197/26.746/26.117 AP-scale points at epochs 81/74/78. Their difference from final test scores must not be interpreted as a quantization improvement. Six holdout training-selection values are retained audit transcriptions, unlike the nine locally retained exploratory training CSVs.

The original train identifier list also contains 78585 and 79029, and the test list contains 35542, 62778, 88586 and 90422, without corresponding entries in the retained annotation JSON. Their JPEG files exist. The converter iterates annotation records, so these six identifiers are outside its retained universe; missing original annotations do not establish either negative-image status or loss of labeled objects during conversion. No labels, splits or checkpoints were changed by this audit. Height endpoints retain COCOeval's inclusive boundaries: 16 final boxes at exactly 96 pixels belong to both the component L and XL ranges. The combined large-like endpoint is evaluated directly and does not double-count these boxes by summing component-bin APs.

\paragraph{Software and materialization.}
The recorded software environment used Linux kernel 7.0.0-28 with glibc 2.39, CUDA 12.8, PyTorch 2.11.0+cu128, Ultralytics 8.4.95, ModelOpt 0.45.0 for the rebuilt COCO engines, ONNX 1.21.0, NumPy 2.4.4, Pillow 12.2.0, OpenCV 5.0.0, NVIDIA driver 610.43.02, and a batch size of one. The compact package does not retain a complete software/build manifest for the older exploratory transfer engines, so it should not be read as documenting every builder flag or package version for those artifacts. Their retained ONNX graphs nevertheless verify the Q/DQ datatype, scale axis, and zero point definitions.

The suite contains four synthetic image corruptions. Gaussian noise draws an independent normal value for each RGB channel with zero mean and $\sigma\in\{8,20,40\}$ on the 0--255 scale, then clips to $[0,255]$ before conversion to 8-bit pixels. Linear motion blur starts with a normalized horizontal line kernel of $\{5,13,23\}$ pixels, rotates it by an integer angle sampled uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,179\}$ degrees, renormalizes it, and applies OpenCV's \texttt{filter2D} with default border extrapolation. Fog creates a random grayscale field on a $\max(2,\lfloor h/32\rfloor)\times\max(2,\lfloor w/32\rfloor)$ grid, bicubically resizes it, applies Gaussian blur, and blends $I'=I(1-sH)+255sH$ with strength--radius pairs $(s,r)$ of $(0.18,6)$, $(0.38,12)$, and $(0.62,20)$. JPEG compression uses quality levels $\{70,40,15\}$ with chroma subsampling disabled. Severity labels 1, 3, and 5 order settings within each corruption but are not physically comparable across types. A SHA-256-derived seed binds dataset, image identifier, corruption, and severity. In the exploratory grid, each stochastic severity receives an independently materialized random field or blur angle; severity summaries are descriptive profiles, not causal dose responses. In the separate realization sensitivity, stochastic fields and blur angles are nested across severity, while deterministic JPEG repeats across labels.

Corrupted outputs and the exploratory matched-clean control are encoded deterministically as JPEG quality 95 with chroma subsampling disabled, and original dimensions are preserved. For the JPEG-corruption arm, the image is first compressed at the named quality and decoded; the result then passes through terminal JPEG-95 encoding. The exploratory clean control skips the named corruption and receives only terminal encoding; the historical final-holdout clean controls instead retain original-source bytes. Each manifest records ordered image identifiers, file hashes and generator parameters. Corruptions are never generated inside an inference worker. For the YOLO comparisons, we fix letterbox geometry, inverse mapping, a confidence threshold of $10^{-3}$, class-wise NMS at IoU 0.7, and a decoder cap of 300 detections per image. COCO-style AP evaluation subsequently uses \texttt{maxDets}=100. Each run record links the engine, decoder, annotations, class map, input manifest, image order, and prediction identity.

\section{Minimum reporting checklist for paired corruption studies}

For reuse of this protocol, a report should state at least:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the exact clean materialization and whether corrupted arms share its
  terminal codec;
  \item the treatment-level quantization recipe, graph coverage, calibration
  identities, runtime version, and decoder;
  \item the atomic image IDs and proof that compared arms consume identical
  ordered inputs;
  \item the direct clean-adjusted contrast, not only corrupted treatment means;
  \item absolute corrupted AP and explicit low-AP inventories;
  \item the resampling unit, pairing structure, draw reuse, and uncertainty
  boundary;
  \item the finite-grid weighting target and all non-pooled endpoint families;
  \item sensitivity to metric scale, split reuse, corruption realization, training and
  calibration seeds, class universe, and recipe transfer where feasible;
  \item for attachment comparisons, the exact aligned graph contract, reused
  controls, and clean/corrupted AP gains; for error diagnostics, the evaluator,
  error-oracle definition, metric scale, and non-additivity boundary.
\end{itemize}
