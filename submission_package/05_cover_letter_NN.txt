Dear Editors of Neural Networks,

Please consider our manuscript, "Quantization fragility under image corruption is recipe-dependent: paired evidence from INT8 and FP8 object detectors," for publication as a research article in Neural Networks.

Deployment of low-precision neural networks raises a reliability question that current evaluation practice answers ambiguously: whether an 8-bit quantized detector remains reliable under corrupted inputs depends on how the engine was built, not only on the datatype label. We make this concrete with a paired, codec-controlled protocol in which every corrupted input is paired with a JPEG-95 clean control and identical encoded bytes reach both precision treatments, and the corruption-associated change in the FP8-INT8 average-precision gap is estimated inside common-image bootstrap samples.

Three findings support the submission. First, under a frozen paired analysis of YOLO11n/m/x on KITTI, VOC, and a 2,000-image COCO subset, the interaction reverses sign across dataset-by-scale cells while FP8 exceeds INT8 on absolute AP in every block mean, so the corruption-induced change in the gap has no format-level ordering on this evidence. Second, a matched-recipe decomposition of RetinaNet localizes a severe INT8 collapse (-21.2 AP on KITTI) to regression-head quantization; excluding that head alone restores clean-competitive accuracy without retraining---the direction replicates on VOC, and both the RetinaNet localization and the contrasting FCOS boundary recur on off-the-shelf COCO-pretrained checkpoints that involve no evaluation-set checkpoint selection, while the 20-AP magnitude is a KITTI estimate that carries a disclosed development-contamination caveat. Third, a controlled two-fold calibration intervention---calibrating INT8 on corruption-augmented images and testing on held-out corruption families under complementary splits, with codec-matched calibration controls---shows the intervention itself is context-dependent (transferring in one fold-by-dataset cell, significantly harming another), so corruption-aware calibration is not supported as a reliable repair and clean-only calibration ranges are not supported as the dominant repairable cause. The paper therefore converts a measurement observation into a validated localization with a practical selective-precision recipe, whose deployment cost is bounded by an idle-gated single-host latency audit rather than left unevaluated.

The work fits Neural Networks' scope on the reliability and deployment behavior of neural network models: it treats quantization as a property of the executable recipe, identifies the fragile network region, and tests a calibrated intervention under frozen decision rules, rather than reporting a format leaderboard.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all six authors have approved its submission.
