\begin{abstract}
Quantized object detectors are deployed on edge accelerators, yet their robustness is commonly judged by comparing number formats on corrupted images alone. Such evaluations confound the clean-image gap with the corruption effect, ignore how each engine is built, and cannot locate or repair a failure. This paper proposes PDR, a paired diagnosis-and-repair framework that overcomes these limitations for executable 8-bit detectors. PDR feeds identical codec-matched bytes to INT8 and FP8 TensorRT engines, estimates the corruption-induced change in their accuracy gap by common-image bootstrap, localizes clean-level deficits by region decomposition and a weight/activation factorial, and repairs them by keeping only the implicated region in floating point, without retraining. Across nine YOLO11 blocks on KITTI, PASCAL VOC, and COCO, the format-level corruption effect reverses sign (\nnHolmSurvivorsWord{} Holm-significant blocks of both signs), although FP8 leads in absolute AP throughout. In RetinaNet, PDR traces a \nnMatchedCleanDeficitKitti-AP INT8 collapse to the activations consumed by the regression head. Selective precision outperforms the standard entropy-calibrated INT8 recipe by \nnHvSelGainPt{} clean-control AP (95\% CI \nnHvSelGainLo{} to \nnHvSelGainHi) on KITTI images unseen by a retrained checkpoint, recovering \nnHvRecovPct\% of the gap to FP8. On the KITTI development set it exceeds the best of \nnRepNCalibWord{} calibration-only repairs by \nnRepSelOverCalibJKitti{} AP, with \nnLatSelVsFpEightMin--\nnLatSelVsFpEightMax\% lower batch-1 engine latency than FP8. Corruption-aware calibration transfers inconsistently and can reduce held-out accuracy. On anchor-free FCOS, neither an INT8 deficit nor a repair gain appears.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Object detectors on edge accelerators rely on post-training quantization (PTQ) to meet memory, energy, and latency budgets~\citep{nagel2021whitepaper,gholami2021survey}. Two 8-bit formats are compared here: 8-bit integer (INT8), with a mature integer inference path~\citep{jacob2018integer,yao2021hawqv3,wu2020integer}, and 8-bit floating point (FP8), which trades mantissa precision for exponent range~\citep{micikevicius2022fp8,shen2024fp8}. Deployed detectors also receive images degraded by noise, blur, weather, and compression, which harm deep visual models~\citep{hendrycks2019corruptions,agarwal2024corruptiondepth} and detectors~\citep{michaelis2019detectorrobustness,liu2024realworldcorruptions,yin2025spatialie}. Selecting a quantized detector for such conditions is a deployment decision; in an analogous forecasting setting, model choices based on a single evaluation reference became unstable under plausible perturbations~\citep{semper2026decisionsupport}.

Current evaluation practice has three limitations. (L1)~Quantized detectors are compared by format label, although an executable detector is defined by a \emph{recipe}: calibration data and preprocessing, quantizer placement, excluded regions, graph transformations, and runtime settings~\citep{vanbaalen2023fp8int8}. (L2)~Robustness is summarized either by the accuracy gap on corrupted images or by retention ratios such as relative performance under corruption (rPC)~\citep{michaelis2019detectorrobustness}. The first combines the clean-image difference with the change caused by corruption; the second measures proportional accuracy preservation within one model and does not estimate how corruption changes the gap between two engines. (L3)~When a quantized detector fails, detector PTQ methods design new quantizers that optimize clean accuracy~\citep{niu2023detptq,ding2024regptq,kim2026inlierq}, and a recent study of degradation-aware INT8 calibration for YOLO detectors~\citep{karimov2025robustness} did not test transfer to corruption families withheld from calibration. Evaluations seldom connect a measured deficit to the model region and operand responsible, and from there to a repair that needs no retraining.

This paper proposes PDR, a paired diagnosis-and-repair framework for executable 8-bit detectors that overcomes these limitations. Stage~1 feeds identical, codec-matched image bytes to the engines under comparison and estimates the corruption-induced change in their average-precision (AP) gap, $\Delta E$, inside common-image bootstrap resamples, which removes the clean-gap confound (L2). Stage~2 localizes a level deficit through region decomposition and an operand factorial that quantizes the weights and activations of a region independently (L1). Stage~3 repairs the deficit by keeping only the implicated region in floating point, with checkpoint and calibration unchanged. Stage~4 verifies the repair on images unseen by a retrained checkpoint, on a second detector family, on independently pretrained checkpoints, and on engine latency. Calibration remedies are compared with the repair on entire corruption families withheld from calibration (L3).

Applied to TensorRT INT8 and FP8 engines of YOLO11, RetinaNet, and FCOS on KITTI, PASCAL VOC, and COCO, PDR yields three main results. First, the format-level corruption effect has no stable sign: across nine YOLO11 dataset$\times$scale blocks, \nnHolmSurvivorsWord{} blocks remain significant after Holm correction, with both signs, although FP8 leads INT8 in clean and corrupt-mean AP in every block. Second, the standard entropy-calibrated INT8 recipe for RetinaNet trails FP8 by \nnMatchedCleanDeficitKitti{} AP on KITTI, and PDR traces this deficit to the activations consumed by the regression head. Third, the selective-precision repair outperforms the standard INT8 recipe by \nnHvSelGainPt{} clean-control AP on KITTI images unseen by a retrained checkpoint, where its difference to FP8 is statistically indistinguishable from zero (\nnHvFinFeSelJ{}). On the KITTI development set it exceeds the best of \nnRepNCalibWord{} calibration-only repairs by \nnRepSelOverCalibJKitti{} clean-control AP, and its batch-1 engine latency is \nnLatSelVsFpEightMin--\nnLatSelVsFpEightMax\% below that of FP8 on the tested GPU. Corruption-aware calibration transfers inconsistently and can significantly reduce held-out accuracy.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.45em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item A paired, codec-controlled estimand with common-image bootstrap inference for executable quantized detectors. It removes the clean-image gap from corrupted-input comparisons and complements retention ratios with an absolute paired interaction.
  \item A localization procedure, region decomposition followed by an operand factorial, that attributes the RetinaNet INT8 deficit to regression-head activations; INT8 head weights cost at most about 0.2 AP.
  \item A retraining-free selective-precision repair that outperforms the standard INT8 recipe by \nnHvSelGainPt{} clean-control AP on a retrained-checkpoint holdout and \nnSelGainJKitti{} AP on KITTI validation, has FP8-minus-selective intervals that include zero on both KITTI sets, and has lower batch-1 engine latency than FP8.
  \item Evidence from two frozen calibration folds that corruption-aware calibration does not transfer reliably to held-out corruption families, together with decision rules for deploying quantized detectors (Section~\ref{sec:decision-rules}).
\end{itemize}

The framework and the experimental protocol are specified in Sections~\ref{sec:framework} and~\ref{sec:setup}; Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the results stage by stage, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses mechanisms, decision rules, and limitations.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Post-training and mixed-precision quantization of detectors}

PTQ estimates numerical ranges from representative data or data-free surrogates and reduces error through clipping, rounding, or local reconstruction~\citep{cai2020zeroq,nagel2020adaround,hubara2021smallcalibration,li2021brecq,wei2022qdrop,liu2023pdquant}. Recent methods address hardware constraints and low-bit vision transformers~\citep{zhao2026haqvit,ding2026raptq,jiang2025adfqvit}. Detectors are harder to quantize than classifiers because classification, localization, feature pyramids, and foreground--background imbalance~\citep{oksuz2021imbalance} must remain calibrated together, and heads that decouple classification from regression~\citep{tian2019fcos,zuo2024tgadhead} give the two tasks separate numerical paths. Q-YOLO targets activation-distribution imbalance in YOLO PTQ~\citep{wang2023qyolo}, DetPTQ and Reg-PTQ address detectors through detection-loss-guided reconstruction and regression-specialized quantizers~\citep{niu2023detptq,ding2024regptq}, and InlierQ suppresses anomalous activations during calibration~\citep{kim2026inlierq}. Reg-PTQ, the closest precedent, identifies regression branches as hard to quantize and proposes a dedicated quantizer~\citep{ding2024regptq}. These methods modify the quantizer and target clean accuracy. The methodological contribution of PDR is different: a clean-gap-adjusted paired comparison of executable engines is coupled with region and operand localization, diagnosis-guided precision selection, and verification, on an unmodified deployment toolchain.

Mixed-precision quantization assigns bit widths by layer sensitivity~\citep{yao2021hawqv3,huang2025hessianmp,sun2026bitflipmpq}; MPQ-YOLO assigns different precisions to the backbone and head of YOLO detectors with training~\citep{liu2024mpqyolo}, and quantization-aware training preserves detection accuracy at low precision at the cost of retraining~\citep{chen2021aqd}. In PDR, precision is assigned from measured regional deficits rather than from a sensitivity proxy: one region stays in floating point, and the checkpoint and calibration budget stay fixed.

\subsection{INT8 versus FP8}

Integer-only inference is well established~\citep{jacob2018integer,yao2021hawqv3,wu2020integer}, and FP8 now has practical inference and PTQ layouts~\citep{micikevicius2022fp8,kuzmin2022fp8,shen2024fp8}. The preferred 8-bit format varies by layer and architecture~\citep{vanbaalen2023fp8int8}, so the datatype alone does not describe an engine. PDR records the full ModelOpt/TensorRT treatment and varies one recipe component at a time, which makes the recipe--format confound measurable.

\subsection{Robustness of quantized and compressed models}

Corruption benchmarks measure aggregate degradation of classifiers~\citep{hendrycks2019corruptions} and detectors~\citep{michaelis2019detectorrobustness,liu2024realworldcorruptions}. Detector-side remedies include image enhancement for adverse weather~\citep{yin2025spatialie} and biologically inspired feature models~\citep{wang2025hubelwiesel}; for classifiers, training-time augmentation~\citep{hendrycks2020augmix} and test-time adaptation~\citep{wang2021tent,niu2022eata,luo2026dpctta} are common. The usual detector summary, $\mathrm{rPC}=\mathrm{mPC}/P_{\mathrm{clean}}$~\citep{michaelis2019detectorrobustness}, with mPC the mean performance under corruption and $P_{\mathrm{clean}}$ the clean performance, measures how much accuracy one model retains.

For quantized models, RobustMQ reports method- and architecture-dependent robustness of quantized classifiers under common corruptions~\citep{xiao2023robustmq}, chaotic noise-injected quantization improves accuracy under adversarial attacks~\citep{osama2024chaotic}, and adversarial robustness under quantization has been studied separately~\citep{li2024quantrobustness}. For detectors, degradation-aware INT8 calibration has been evaluated across YOLO scales with inconsistent gains~\citep{karimov2025robustness}, and detection-discriminant reorganization combines compression with robustness~\citep{choi2026detectorrobustness}. More broadly, TTAQ couples perturbation-aware PTQ with adaptation under continual shift~\citep{xiao2024ttaq}, and Recti-Q rectifies out-of-distribution features of quantized perception models~\citep{araghi2026rectiq}. These approaches address quantization, compression, or adaptation. In PDR, identical-input engine comparisons are coupled to regional diagnosis; the paired interaction is reported together with absolute AP, so that a gap shrinking near a shared failure floor is not read as robustness, and calibration transfer is tested on entire corruption families withheld from calibration.

\section{Proposed PDR framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Overview}

\FloatBarrier
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_framework.pdf}
\caption{The PDR framework. (a)~Four stages: paired diagnosis, localization, selective-precision repair, and verification. (b)~Stage-1 protocol: each source image yields a clean control re-encoded at JPEG quality 95 ($\Jclean$) and corrupted inputs that are also re-encoded at quality 95, so the INT8 and FP8 engines receive identical bytes. The four AP cells give the clean gap $G_0$ and the corrupted gap $G_c$, and $\Delta E=G_c-G_0$. The FP32 reference cancels from $\Delta E$ and is not shown.}
\label{fig:framework}
\end{figure*}

PDR (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:pdr}) takes a trained detector, its candidate 8-bit recipes, and a corruption suite, and returns a per-region diagnosis, a repaired recipe, and its verified accuracy and latency. A \emph{treatment} is the complete recorded configuration of an executable detector: checkpoint, Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) graph, quantize/dequantize (Q/DQ) placement, calibration list and preprocessing, excluded operators, TensorRT builder settings, runtime, and decoder. ``INT8'' and ``FP8'' therefore qualify treatments, not isolated datatypes; an \emph{arm} is one treatment within an experiment. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pdr} is a retrospective statement of the workflow, not an automated search: the comparisons of each stage were designed by hand, and Section~\ref{sec:evidence-status} gives the order in which they were run and their inferential status.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Paired diagnosis and selective-precision repair (PDR), stated as a retrospective workflow of the analyses reported here}
\label{alg:pdr}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require FP32 detector; INT8 and FP8 recipes; evaluation images $I$; corruption cells $C$; calibration images disjoint from $I$; holdout images $H$
\Ensure Region diagnosis, repaired recipe, verified AP and latency
\State Match calibration preprocessing to inference byte for byte; build all arms from one ONNX graph
\ForAll{$i\in I$, $x\in\{\Jclean\}\cup C$}
  \State Encode $i$ under $x$ with terminal JPEG-95; run every arm on the identical bytes
\EndFor
\State \textbf{Stage 1:} on one shared bootstrap schedule, estimate $G_0$, $G_c$, and $\Delta E=G_c-G_0$; report them with absolute AP
\If{the INT8 arm shows a clean-level deficit to FP8}
  \State \textbf{Stage 2:} compare targeted arms that quantize only one region or all but one region (backbone, FPN, classification head, regression head)
  \If{one region reproduces the deficit when quantized alone and removes it when excluded}
    \State $r^\star\gets$ that region
    \State Quantize the weights and activations of $r^\star$ separately (W-only, A-only) to identify the implicated operand
    \State \textbf{Stage 3:} exclude $r^\star$ from Q/DQ insertion; keep checkpoint and calibration fixed
  \Else
    \State Report the deficit as unlocalized; retain FP8 (fallback, not exercised here)
  \EndIf
\Else
  \State Keep the INT8 recipe; report $\Delta E$ with absolute AP
\EndIf
\State \textbf{Stage 4:} if a repaired recipe exists, re-estimate repaired$-$INT8 and FP8$-$repaired contrasts on $H$ and on further detector families; measure batch-1 engine latency
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Stage 1: paired corruption estimand}
\label{sec:estimands}

For dataset $d$, model $m$, treatment $q$, and condition $c$, $\AP_{d,m,q,c}$ denotes COCO-style AP@[.5:.95] in AP points, and $\Jclean$ denotes the clean control, i.e., the source image encoded at JPEG quality 95 without corruption. The FP8--INT8 gaps on clean and corrupted inputs are
\begin{equation}
G_{d,m,x}=\AP_{d,m,\mathrm{FP8},x}-\AP_{d,m,\mathrm{INT8},x},
\quad x\in\{\Jclean,c\},
\end{equation}
and the primary interaction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{d,m,c}=G_{d,m,c}-G_{d,m,\Jclean}.
\label{eq:delta-e}
\end{equation}
A positive $\Delta E$ indicates that the FP8--INT8 gap widens under corruption, and a negative value that it narrows; the FP32 reference cancels. With $G_0=G_{d,m,\Jclean}$ and $G_{\mathrm{corr}}$ the mean of $G_{d,m,c}$ over the 12 corruption--severity cells, the per-block estimand is $\Delta E=G_{\mathrm{corr}}-G_0$. The contrast has the algebra of a difference-in-differences interaction and is read descriptively, without a causal claim. $\Delta E$ is scale-sensitive: if both treatments retain the same fraction $r$ of their clean AP, then $\Delta E=(r-1)G_0$, which is negative whenever $G_0>0$ and $r<1$. Every $\Delta E$ is therefore reported with the clean gap and the absolute corrupted AP (the floor guard).

The same construction applies to two arms of one format (for example, INT8-selective minus INT8-matched), which isolates a single recipe component. Restricting the corrupted set to a subset $S$ gives the calibration contrast
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{A-B,\,S}=\big[\AP_A-\AP_B\big]_{S}-\big[\AP_A-\AP_B\big]_{\Jclean},
\label{eq:delta-cal}
\end{equation}
denoted $\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}$ or $\Delta E_{\text{in-family}}$; for a calibration-variant-minus-baseline contrast, a positive value is a corruption-specific benefit. The \emph{level deficit} of an arm is its AP shortfall to a comparator on clean inputs ($G_0$ when the comparator is FP8); the interaction $\Delta E$ is what corruption adds. The RetinaNet INT8-matched arm, for example, has a large level deficit but a negative $\Delta E$ on KITTI.

\subsection{Stage 2: region and operand localization}
\label{sec:localization}

A level deficit is localized in two steps. \emph{Region decomposition} builds INT8 arms that quantize one region only (backbone, feature pyramid network (FPN), classification head, or regression head, alone or combined) and arms that quantize everything except one region. Their $\Jclean$ AP is compared with the FP32 and FP8 references, and recipe sweeps over preprocessing, range estimator, calibration budget, and Q/DQ placement test whether the split depends on these choices. The region $r^\star$ is the one whose quantization alone reproduces the deficit and whose exclusion alone removes it.

The \emph{operand factorial} then quantizes only one operand class of $r^\star$: INT8 weights with fp16 activations (W-only) and fp16 weights with INT8 activations (A-only). Only the Q/DQ edges of $r^\star$ are rewired, and each rewired edge is checked against the selective graph. With the fully quantized and selective arms, this gives a $2{\times}2$ factorial over weights and activations, with interaction selective$-$W-only$-$A-only$+$matched. Rewiring acts on the consumer side, so ``head activations'' include the FPN feature maps that enter the head.

\subsection{Stage 3: selective-precision repair}
\label{sec:selective}

The repaired recipe, \emph{INT8-selective}, excludes every node of $r^\star$ from Q/DQ insertion, so these convolutions have 16-bit floating-point (fp16) operands in the graph, while all other quantizers, the checkpoint, the calibration list, and the builder settings stay identical to the INT8 recipe. For RetinaNet, $r^\star$ is the \texttt{/head/regression\_head/} subgraph; for FCOS, the same exclusion also covers the centerness output, which torchvision places in the regression head. The repair requires no retraining, no additional data, and no change to the TensorRT toolchain.

\subsection{Stage 4: verification and statistical inference}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

Intervals describe uncertainty from the finite set of evaluation images, with checkpoints, engines, calibration, and corruptions fixed~\citep{bouthillier2021variance,pineau2021reproducibility}. Within each dataset$\times$model block, one deterministic schedule of 2,000 image resamples~\citep{efron1979bootstrap} is shared by all arms and conditions, so every contrast is computed on common resamples. Point estimates are full-sample values; intervals are 2.5th--97.5th bootstrap percentiles, marked \sigstar{} when they exclude zero. Two-sided $p$-values use the plus-one rule $\widetilde p=\min\{1,\,2(b_{\min}{+}1)/(B{+}1)\}$, where $b_{\min}$ is the smaller of the numbers of draws $\le0$ and $\ge0$, and are Holm-adjusted across the nine YOLO11 blocks~\citep{holm1979simple}. Heterogeneity across blocks is tested with Cochran's $Q$~\citep{cochran1954combination,higgins2002quantifying} and, because blocks of one dataset share images, with a generalized $Q$ that uses the covariance from a rerun with a dataset-shared schedule. KITTI frames are correlated within drives, so KITTI intervals are also recomputed by resampling the \nnClDrives{} drives of the validation partition ($B=1{,}000$; Supplement S9).

Verification has four parts: a KITTI holdout scored with a retrained checkpoint (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}), a second detector family (FCOS), off-the-shelf COCO-pretrained checkpoints, and deployment checks of batch-1 latency and engine-rebuild determinism (Supplement S12). Diagnostic arms are single runs without intervals.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Detectors, engines, and corruptions}
\label{sec:corruptions}

YOLO11n/m/x~\citep{jocher2024yolo11}, RetinaNet with a ResNet-50 FPN~\citep{lin2017focal,torchvision2026retinanet}, and anchor-free FCOS~\citep{tian2019fcos,torchvision2026fcos} are evaluated. All engines were built with TensorRT 11.1.0.106~\citep{nvidia2026tensorrt} on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090. Quantized graphs were produced by NVIDIA ModelOpt with entropy calibration~\citep{nvidia2026modelopt,migacz2017entropy}, except the max-calibration baselines of Section~\ref{sec:intervention-design}; study-built registries record ModelOpt 0.45.0, and the older YOLO11 registries do not record its version. The YOLO11 engines predate the frozen analysis and were built by \texttt{trtexec} with TensorRT defaults (TensorFloat-32, TF32, enabled); the RetinaNet and FCOS quantized arms were built with the TensorRT Python builder and TF32 disabled. Within each family, all quantized arms entering a contrast share one TF32 setting. Paired and calibration arms use 512 deterministically selected training images, disjoint from the evaluation images.

The RetinaNet arms are \textbf{INT8-legacy} (the historical recipe, whose calibration preprocessing differs from inference; diagnostic only), \textbf{INT8-matched} (the standard entropy INT8 recipe with calibration preprocessing identical to inference), \textbf{INT8-selective} (Section~\ref{sec:selective}), the calibration baselines of Section~\ref{sec:intervention-design}, \textbf{FP8-matched}, and \textbf{FP32}. Supplement S4 gives the full arm registry and terminology.

Four synthetic corruptions are generated deterministically at three severities (labels 1/3/5): Gaussian noise ($\sigma\in\{8,20,40\}$ on the 0--255 scale), linear motion blur (kernel lengths $\{5,13,23\}$, random angle), fog (strength--radius pairs $(0.18,6)$, $(0.38,12)$, $(0.62,20)$), and JPEG compression (qualities $\{70,40,15\}$). Every output is re-encoded at JPEG quality 95, so clean controls and corrupted inputs share the final codec, and a SHA-256-derived seed binds dataset, image, corruption, and severity. Inference uses a 640-pixel letterbox and family-specific decoders: YOLO11 uses a confidence threshold of 0.001 and class-wise non-maximum suppression (NMS) at an intersection-over-union (IoU) of 0.7; RetinaNet uses 0.05 and batched NMS at IoU 0.5; both keep at most 300 detections. AP follows the COCO protocol (\texttt{pycocotools}, \texttt{maxDets}=100).

\subsection{Datasets and checkpoints}
\label{sec:datasets}

Three evaluation sets are used: KITTI validation (1,496 images)~\citep{geiger2012kitti}, PASCAL VOC2007 test (4,952 images)~\citep{everingham2015voc}, and a fixed 2,000-image subset of COCO val2017~\citep{lin2014coco} (seed 20260924). VOC and KITTI annotations were converted from Ultralytics YOLO labels to COCO format; VOC objects marked difficult are omitted, and KITTI keeps eight categories and drops \texttt{DontCare} regions. KITTI and VOC YOLO11 models were fine-tuned from Ultralytics weights; RetinaNet and FCOS ResNet-50-FPN models were fine-tuned for 100 epochs at 640-pixel input (seed 20260807). Checkpoints were selected on the evaluation partition, so absolute KITTI and VOC AP is optimistic; whether selection affects the two arms of a contrast differently is unknown. COCO YOLO11 uses the official checkpoint. Each block covers original clean images, the codec control, and 12 corruption cells (14 conditions per arm; 13 for the older RetinaNet FP32 and legacy arms, which predate the codec control). All checkpoint, split, and training manifests are hash-bound (Supplement S4).

The KITTI evaluation set is the 1,496-image Ultralytics validation partition of the 7,481-image training set, and all KITTI checkpoints of the main experiments were trained on the complementary 5,985 images. For an unseen-image test, RetinaNet was retrained with the same profile on 4,788 of these images and selected on the 1,496-image set; the remaining 1,197 images form the holdout. For the retrained checkpoint, the holdout images were excluded from training, checkpoint selection, and calibration, but the recipes were developed earlier on checkpoints that had seen them. KITTI is split by frame, so drives cross every partition boundary: 3\% of the 512 calibration images (13/512) have an identical-hash evaluation frame under a 16${\times}$16 average hash, and 12\% have a match within distance~5. The holdout is therefore image-disjoint but not sequence-disjoint; it is analyzed with both the image and the drive-clustered bootstrap. The KITTI validation set was used while the selective recipe was developed, so KITTI-validation selective estimates are development-contaminated; VOC, the COCO-pretrained replication, and the retrained holdout are cleaner checks.

\subsection{Baseline repairs}
\label{sec:intervention-design}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{nn_fold_design.pdf}
\caption{Corruption-aware calibration folds and the codec-matched control arm. Filled blue cells are families seen by entropy calibration (the in-family partition); hatched red cells are held out: their named corruption settings never enter calibration, although every calibration image carries the JPEG-95 encoding. Fold~1 (INT8-corruptcalib) calibrates Gaussian noise and JPEG and is tested on fog and motion blur; fold~2 (INT8-cc2calib) swaps the roles. INT8-q95calib sees only the same 512 clean images re-encoded at JPEG quality~95 and is evaluated on both partitions. Selective-tier arms (sel-corruptcalib, sel-cc2calib, sel-q95calib) share each fold's split.}
\label{fig:folds}
\end{figure*}

Selective precision is compared with five retraining-free repairs that change only calibration.

\emph{Corruption-aware calibration.} The calibration baseline tests one hypothesis under decision rules frozen before any of its runs: \emph{if INT8-matched corrupted-input accuracy is limited because calibration images never show corrupted activation ranges, calibration on corrupted images should recover AP on held-out corruptions.} Each of the 512 calibration images is assigned by a hash of its path to one of six in-family cells and corrupted with the evaluation parameters and JPEG-95 encoding. Fold~1 calibrates on Gaussian noise and JPEG and holds out fog and motion blur; fold~2 swaps the roles (75--102 images per cell; Fig.~\ref{fig:folds}). The full tier re-quantizes INT8-matched, and the selective tier re-quantizes INT8-selective. The fold-1 primary criterion requires the held-out corrupt mean of the full-tier arm to exceed that of INT8-matched with an interval above zero; the fold-2 replication rule is registered two-sidedly and is read through the directional fold-1 criterion it cites. Fold~2 also requires $\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}>0$ against INT8-matched and against the codec control. Supplement S6 lists every rule and its registration status. In-family gains alone indicate calibration fitting, not transfer, and all rules are reported regardless of outcome.

\emph{Codec-matched calibration.} Corrupt-calibration images carry the JPEG-95 encoding of the evaluation images, whereas the standard calibration list uses the original files. The INT8-q95calib controls, calibrated on the same 512 clean images re-encoded at quality 95, separate codec adaptation from corruption coverage. They are post hoc for fold~1 and pre-specified for fold~2.

\emph{Max calibration.} INT8-matched and INT8-selective were rebuilt with max instead of entropy calibration. In a more local variant, the scale of every activation quantizer consumed by the regression head was set to the calibration-set maximum of its tensor, with all other scales unchanged. Five of these quantizers sit on FPN inputs shared with the classification head, so the patch is local to the regression tower but not at the shared boundary. The patch is scripted (scale $=\mathrm{fp16}(|x|_{\max}/127)$); it reproduces the archived KITTI graph byte for byte and the nodes and initializers of the VOC graph exactly. Both variants are single builds reported as point estimates (Supplement S14).

\subsection{Evidence status}
\label{sec:evidence-status}

The analyses were run in a different order from the stages. Diagnostic single-run decompositions on KITTI identified the regression head and the selective recipe first; the estimands, condition schedule, and bootstrap plan for evaluating the already chosen YOLO11 and RetinaNet arms were then frozen; the operand factorial and the retrained holdout followed. The freeze therefore covers the Stage-1 and Stage-3 evaluation, not the discovery of the repair or the four-stage workflow, and the YOLO11 engines predate it. The calibration folds were frozen before their runs. The region decomposition is diagnostic: single development-set builds that guided the design. The operand factorial, the FCOS and COCO-pretrained replications, the retrained holdout, the max-calibration counterfactuals, and the activation, latency, and rebuild audits were specified after the paired freeze and are reported regardless of outcome. Holm adjustment covers the nine YOLO11 blocks, a family chosen at analysis time; other intervals are unadjusted. An earlier 144-cell exploratory YOLO11 analysis motivated the frozen design and is reported only in Supplement S10. Supplement S10 tabulates the inferential status of every layer.

\subsection{Stage 1: the format-level corruption interaction has no stable sign}
\label{sec:yolo-results}

\begin{figure*}[t]

Table~\ref{tab:yolo-deltae} and Fig.~\ref{fig:forest} report the nine YOLO11 blocks. FP8 exceeds INT8 in clean-control AP in every block ($G_0$ from \nnGZeroMin{} to \nnGZeroMax{} AP) and in the 12-cell corrupt mean, although a few individual cells reverse the order. The change in this gap under corruption, however, has no stable sign: $\Delta E$ is positive on VOC/YOLO11n (\nnDEciVocN) and COCO-2k/YOLO11m (\nnDEciCocoM), negative on VOC/YOLO11m (\nnDEciVocM), KITTI/YOLO11m (\nnDEciKittiM), KITTI/YOLO11x (\nnDEciKittiX), and COCO-2k/YOLO11x (\nnDEciCocoX), and compatible with zero elsewhere (unadjusted). On VOC alone, the sign changes with model scale ($\nnDEVocN$, $\nnDEVocM$, and $\nnDEVocX$ for n, m, and x).

Cochran's test rejects a common $\Delta E$ ($Q=\nnHetQ$, $\nnHetDf$ df, $p\approx\nnHetP$; $I^2=\nnHetIsq\%$; $\tau=\nnHetTau$ AP). Accounting for the shared images within a dataset through the covariance of a shared-schedule rerun ($B=\nnHetGlsDraws$) leaves the result unchanged (generalized $Q=\nnHetGlsQ$, $p\approx\nnHetGlsP$). Heterogeneity also holds within VOC ($Q=\nnHetGlsQVoc$, $p\approx\nnHetGlsPVoc$) and COCO ($Q=\nnHetGlsQCoco$, $p\approx\nnHetGlsPCoco$), where only model scale varies, but not within KITTI ($Q=\nnHetGlsQKitti$, $p=\nnHetGlsPKitti$). After Holm adjustment, \nnHolmSurvivors{} blocks remain significant with \emph{both} signs: COCO-2k/YOLO11m is positive, and KITTI/YOLO11x, VOC/YOLO11m, and COCO-2k/YOLO11x are negative. The DerSimonian--Laird mean of $\nnReMean$ AP (\nnReMeanCi) is only a descriptive average.

Heterogeneity also appears across corruption families within blocks (Supplement S9); on VOC/YOLO11x, for instance, JPEG contributes $\nnFamVocXJpeg$~AP whereas Gaussian noise and motion blur contribute $\nnFamVocXGn$ and $\nnFamVocXMb$~AP. Removing the saturated motion-blur-s5 cell preserves the sign of every Holm-significant block ($Q\approx\nnHetQEx$). Under the drive-clustered bootstrap, KITTI/YOLO11x remains negative (\nnClYoloXKitti), whereas KITTI/YOLO11m becomes compatible with zero (\nnClYoloMKitti). The codec control behaves as intended: $\Jclean$ AP stays within \nnJCleanFid{} AP of original clean AP for every arm, and within-model retention ranges from \nnRetMin{} to \nnRetMax{}, with INT8 and FP8 differing by at most \nnRetDiff. Retention-based summaries would therefore rate the two formats as equally robust, whereas the paired estimand reveals significant interactions of both signs. With the recipe fixed, the interaction depends on dataset and model, and no format-level rule predicts it.

Graph position does not explain the split, because the equally terminal classification head shows no measurable loss; nor does the number of quantized sites, because the restricted Conv+Add placement (\nnDiagConvAddJ) quantizes fewer sites than full INT8 yet does worse.

\subsection{Mechanism of the regression-head deficit}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{AP by IoU threshold and object size, RetinaNet, $\Jclean$ basis (single runs). W-only: INT8 head weights, fp16 head activations; A-only: the reverse. Bold: AP$_{75}$ and AP$_S$ of the arms with INT8 head activations, where the deficit concentrates.}
\label{tab:iou-size}
\footnotesize
{\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3.5pt}\input{generated/nn_iou_size.tex}}
\end{table}

The decomposition and the paired selective arm agree that the regression head carries the deficit, in line with detector PTQ work on regression branches~\citep{ding2024regptq}. RetinaNet decodes width and height offsets through an exponential, so a fixed error on the raw output becomes a proportional box error that high-IoU AP penalizes, whereas classification scores pass through a saturating sigmoid. On KITTI the INT8-matched gap to FP8 is \nnIouGapSeventyFiveKitti{} AP at $\mathrm{AP}_{75}$ against \nnIouGapFiftyKitti{} AP at $\mathrm{AP}_{50}$, and $\mathrm{AP}_S$ falls to \nnIouApSmallInKitti{} against \nnIouApSmallFeKitti{}, while $\mathrm{AP}_L$ drops by only \nnIouGapLargeKitti{} AP (Table~\ref{tab:iou-size}). A-only reproduces this profile and W-only does not. Size-stratified AP also depends on baseline localization, so this profile cannot by itself separate decoding sensitivity from activation quantization.

Activation captures and the Q/DQ structure refine this picture (Supplement~S13). Each FPN level has its own per-tensor activation scale at the regression head, so the deficit is not caused by one range shared across levels. The tails, however, are heaviest at the finest levels: median excess kurtosis is \nnActRegKurtPThreeKitti{} and \nnActRegKurtPFourKitti{} at P3 and P4 against \nnActRegKurtPSevenKitti{} at P7 on KITTI, compared with \nnActClsKurtPThreeKitti{} and \nnActClsKurtPFourKitti{} for the classification head, with the same ordering on VOC and a P4 peak on the pretrained COCO graph. Heavier fine-level tails are consistent with coarser INT8 resolution near the center of the distribution at the levels that predict small objects, which would match the $\mathrm{AP}_S$ and $\mathrm{AP}_{75}$ collapse. FCOS regression-head kurtosis at P3 and P4 (\nnActRegKurtPThreeFcosKitti{} and \nnActRegKurtPFourFcosKitti{} on KITTI) is far below the RetinaNet values, consistent with its null. The max-calibration results are compatible with a grid-resolution account, and FP8, whose exponent field provides a wide dynamic range, quantizes the same head without failing (\nnArmFeCleanKitti{} AP on KITTI). Because max calibration changes clipping and resolution together, the two mechanisms are not isolated, and this is a distributional argument, not a causal test. Why head exclusion helps about eight times more on KITTI than on VOC (\nnSelGainJKitti{} versus \nnSelGainJVoc{} AP) remains open; differences in object-size distributions are one candidate.

\subsection{Decision rules for deploying quantized detectors}
\label{sec:decision-rules}

The evidence supports five rules.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.9em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
\item Calibration preprocessing should match inference byte for byte before any quantization gap is interpreted; on KITTI this alone recovered \nnDiagPreprocGain{} AP in the diagnostic builds.
\item Format comparisons under corruption should use the paired, clean-gap-adjusted estimand with absolute corrupted AP, because the FP8--INT8 interaction changed sign between dataset and scale blocks of a single detector family while retention ratios were nearly equal.
\item Regression and classification heads should be evaluated as separate quantization regions; when one region carries the deficit, selective precision is the retraining-free repair to try first, since it recovered \nnHvRecovPct\% of the clean-control FP8--INT8 gap on unseen images with lower batch-1 engine latency than FP8.
\item When INT8 shows no deficit relative to FP8 and head exclusion brings no gain, as for FCOS, full INT8 should be kept, because selective precision then adds latency without an accuracy gain.
\item Corruption-augmented calibration should not be expected to transfer; its held-out effect ranged from a significant harm to a $\nnCtHVocP$-AP gain, and calibration images should share the deployment codec before gains are credited to corruption coverage.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The paired and calibration experiments use one checkpoint per dataset and model, plus a retrained KITTI RetinaNet checkpoint. Each arm has one engine build and one 512-image calibration draw. The paired experiments use entropy calibration, with max calibration examined only in single builds; testing is otherwise confined to the recorded ModelOpt and TensorRT 11.1 recipes and one RTX 5090; other PTQ methods may distribute quantization error differently. The mechanism analysis covers one anchor-based and one anchor-free family. Intervals cover image sampling only, not training-seed, calibration-draw, or corruption-realization variance. The drive-clustered bootstrap changes KITTI interval widths by a factor of \nnClWidthMin--\nnClWidthMax{} and the significance of \nnClChanged{} of \nnClClaims{} KITTI contrasts, all with an image-bootstrap bound near zero; the RetinaNet deficit, its selective repair, the operand factorial, and the calibration verdicts keep their significance. Holm adjustment covers only the nine YOLO11 blocks; for the 56 FCOS contrasts, the $p$-value resolution at $B=2{,}000$ prevents any Holm rejection, so they remain exploratory. The null-seeking selective criterion has no equivalence margin, so an interval containing zero does not prove equivalence. Checkpoints were selected on the KITTI and VOC evaluation partitions; the retrained holdout avoids this for RetinaNet but is split by frame, not by drive. The corruption suite is synthetic, with four types whose severities are not comparable across types, and the calibration experiment tests two folds of these types; on RetinaNet the $\Jclean$ control differs from original clean images by up to $\nnRetJCleanFid$ AP, enough to move small residuals across significance thresholds; the fold split confounds corruption family with frequency content, held-out JPEG is not fully unseen because all calibration images are JPEG-encoded, and real-world shifts are untested. COCO evidence uses a 2,000-image subset, and the pretrained replication covers one backbone.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

PDR overcomes the limitations of format-level robustness evaluation identified in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}. Its paired, codec-controlled estimand shows that the FP8--INT8 corruption interaction of YOLO11 has no stable sign across datasets and scales, although FP8 has higher absolute AP in every block, so the format label alone does not predict corruption behavior. Its localization stage traces the severe RetinaNet INT8 deficit to the activations consumed by the regression head, with INT8 head weights costing at most about 0.2 AP. Its selective-precision repair outperforms the standard INT8 recipe by \nnHvSelGainPt{} clean-control AP on images unseen by a retrained checkpoint and by \nnSelGainJKitti{} AP on KITTI validation, exceeds every calibration-only repair tested, is statistically indistinguishable from FP8 on both KITTI sets (equivalence was not tested; a small residual remains on VOC), and has lower batch-1 engine latency than FP8, without retraining. On FCOS, the same procedure detects no INT8 deficit relative to FP8 and no gain from regression-head exclusion, so plain INT8 is retained. Corruption-aware calibration, by contrast, transfers inconsistently and is not a reliable repair. Future work will apply PDR to transformer detectors, other toolchains, and real-world shifts, and will separate clipping from grid resolution in the regression head.
