\section{Purpose and scope}

This supplement documents the frozen protocol details, estimands, corruption
parameters, calibration schedules, and artifact contract supporting the main
article. All reported values are generated deterministically from retained
ledgers. Section~S8 additionally reports per-family $\Delta E$ diagnostics that
support---but do not extend---the main-text claims.

\section{Estimands}

For dataset $d$, model $m$, corruption condition $c$, and a pair of treatments
(A, B) sharing the same evaluator:

\begin{align}
G_0 &= \AP_A(\Jclean) - \AP_B(\Jclean), \\
G_{\mathrm{corr}} &= \overline{\AP_A(c) - \AP_B(c)} \quad\text{(equal weight over the 12 cells)}, \\
\Delta E &= G_{\mathrm{corr}} - G_0 .
\end{align}

$\Jclean$ denotes the codec-matched clean control: the $\Jclean$ clean control
and every corrupted input are terminally encoded as JPEG quality 95 with
chroma subsampling disabled, so the clean control and the corrupted inputs
differ only in the named corruption transform, not in container encoding; the
separate original-clean cell keeps the dataset-native bytes; it checks
control fidelity and anchors the clean-accuracy comparisons behind the
RetinaNet collapse and recovery claims, while the paired interaction uses
the $\Jclean$ basis. $\Delta E > 0$ means the A-minus-B gap widens under
corruption; $\Delta E < 0$ means it narrows. A narrowing gap near a shared
accuracy floor is not read as robustness: absolute corrupted AP is reported
alongside every interaction (main text, \S4.1--\S4.3).

\section{Frozen condition schedule}

Each dataset$\times$model block evaluates 14 conditions on identical image
ordering for quantized arms (13 for the RetinaNet FP32 and INT8-legacy arms
that predate codec control):

\begin{itemize}
  \item clean (original bytes) and $\Jclean$ (JPEG-95 clean control);
  \item Gaussian noise: $\sigma \in \{8, 20, 40\}$;
  \item motion blur: kernel lengths $\{5, 13, 23\}$;
  \item synthetic fog: (strength, radius) $\in \{(0.18,6), (0.38,12), (0.62,20)\}$;
  \item JPEG compression: qualities $\{70, 40, 15\}$.
\end{itemize}

{\sloppy Corruption parameters and the JPEG-95 terminal encoding are frozen in
versioned configuration \texttt{corruptions.json}, whose SHA-256 is recorded
as \texttt{generator\_config\_sha256} in the corruption-aware and codec-control
calibration manifests; the
frozen protocol file \texttt{nn\_paired\_protocol\_v1\_20260924} fixes the
condition list and evaluation contract on top of that generator. The same
transform code produces both the evaluation caches and the
corruption-aware calibration images.\par}

\section{Inference and evaluation contract}

YOLO11 blocks: 640-pixel letterbox input, confidence threshold 0.001,
class-wise NMS at IoU 0.7, at most 300 detections. RetinaNet blocks: 640-pixel
letterbox input, confidence threshold 0.05, batched NMS at IoU 0.5, at most
300 detections. FCOS blocks: 640-pixel letterbox input, confidence threshold
0.05 on the centerness-weighted score, at most 1,000 pre-NMS candidates per
feature level, batched NMS at IoU 0.5, at most 300 detections---the
RetinaNet constants reused post hoc and frozen for FCOS at the RetinaNet
values, and not torchvision's FCOS defaults
(score threshold 0.2, NMS IoU 0.6, 100 detections per image); the decoder is
a reimplementation of torchvision's post-processing, validated detection-for-detection
against torchvision's own pipeline on 32 seeded synthetic head-output
draws
(\texttt{phase\_h\_fcos\_results/decoder\_validation.json}: 9,600
detections, maximum box and score deviation exactly zero). The check
saturates the 300-detection cap on every draw, uses a square 640-pixel
input at scale 1.0, and covers the 8-class KITTI head only---it validates
the post-processing arithmetic, not letterbox un-mapping, sparse-output
behavior, or end-to-end engine parity. All evaluations use the COCO protocol via
\texttt{pycocotools} with \texttt{maxDets=100}; VOC and KITTI annotations were
converted once to COCO format and frozen. Decoder constants are listed per family in
the paragraph above---identical for RetinaNet and FCOS---and held fixed
within each; cross-family comparisons are
comparisons of recipe bundles, never of formats alone.

\section{Corruption-aware calibration schedule}

The intervention calibration sets reuse the same 512 images as the matched
clean-calibration manifests. Each image is deterministically assigned---via a
SHA-256-keyed hash of its identifier---to exactly one of six in-family cells,
Gaussian noise or JPEG at severities 1, 3, or 5 (approximately 77--102 images
per cell). Assigned corruptions use the frozen transform parameters above, and
the outputs are terminally encoded at JPEG quality 95, matching the evaluation
input contract. In this first fold fog and motion blur never appear in
calibration and form the held-out split. A complementary second fold (schedule
\texttt{nn-corruptcalib-fold2-v1}, frozen before any of its runs) assigns the
same images to fog or motion blur at severities 1, 3, or 5 (75--95 images per
cell) and holds out Gaussian noise and JPEG. Its pre-specified rules are
(i) a held-out change from INT8-matched whose interval excludes zero
(either direction counts; the main text reads it through the directional
fold-1 criterion) and
(ii) a positive held-out $\Delta E$ with an interval excluding zero, against
both INT8-matched and INT8-q95calib.

The codec-matched control manifests encode the same 512 images at JPEG
quality 95 with no named corruption, isolating codec adaptation from
corruption coverage.

\section{Paired bootstrap}

Within each dataset$\times$model block, one deterministic schedule of
$B=2{,}000$ resamples of image indices is shared across all arms and all
conditions, so every contrast is evaluated inside common-image samples.
Resamples index evaluated images by position, so a duplicated image
contributes its detections and ground truth once per occurrence; this
multiplicity is verified by a dedicated unit test
(\texttt{tests/test\_bootstrap\_multiplicity.py}), which constructs a
stub evaluation where resample $[0,0,1]$ scores $34/101$ against the
$51/101$ that deduplication would give. Reported point estimates are
full-sample plug-in values; reported intervals are the 2.5th--97.5th
percentiles of the bootstrap distribution of each contrast. Intervals cover
image resampling only; they do not cover training-seed, calibration-draw,
engine-build, or corruption-realization variance, and image-level resampling
treats frames as independent (KITTI frames from shared driving sequences are
correlated, so those intervals may be optimistic).

Two-sided bootstrap $p$-values use a consistent plus-one convention in each
tail, $\widetilde p=\min\{1,\,2(b_{\min}+1)/(B+1)\}$, so the smallest
attainable value at $B=2{,}000$ is $2/2001\approx0.001$ and no $p$ is ever
exactly zero; Holm adjustment covers the nine paired YOLO11 blocks. Heterogeneity of
$\Delta E$ across those blocks uses Cochran's
$Q=\sum_i w_i(\hat\Delta_i-\bar\Delta_w)^2$ with $w_i=1/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_i^2$
from the bootstrap draws, $I^2=\max\{0,(Q-\mathrm{df})/Q\}$, and the
DerSimonian--Laird between-block standard deviation $\tau$. Blocks within a
dataset share images, so their estimates may be correlated; the cross-block
covariance is not identifiable from the phase-B caches because each block's
resample schedule is seeded per dataset$\times$model. To bound the induced
dependence we re-ran the nine YOLO11 blocks with $B=1{,}000$ under a
schedule seeded per dataset only (\texttt{shared\_schedule/bootstrap/}):
per-draw Pearson correlations of the FP8$-$INT8 $\Delta E$ between model
scales are $r=\{0.240, 0.213, -0.104\}$ on KITTI (n$\sim$m, n$\sim$x,
m$\sim$x), $\{-0.033, -0.066, 0.111\}$ on VOC, and $\{0.057, 0.013, -0.196\}$
on the COCO subset---small and mixed-sign, so treating the blocks as
independent appears only modestly optimistic for the pooled statistics (no
generalized $Q$ with a full covariance matrix was computed) and does
not affect the per-block Holm adjustment, which requires valid marginal
$p$-values but no independence among tests. RetinaNet and
intervention intervals are per contrast.

As a cross-machine check, the pretrained-FCOS block was bootstrapped
independently on two hosts under the same seed namespace
(\texttt{coco\_\_fcos\_pretrained}): the 56 cell-level draw arrays agree to
$2.2\times10^{-16}$ absolute difference (identical seed, $B$, and image
set), so resample schedules are portable; container file hashes still
differ because NPZ serialization embeds metadata.

Point estimates are full-sample plug-in values computed from the metric
records of each cell (for COCO, from the frozen 2,000-image subset
evaluation); bootstrap draws supply intervals, standard errors, and
$p$-values only. For the intervention, the corruption-specific component on
the held-out family is
$\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}=(\AP_A-\AP_B)_{\text{held-out}}-(\AP_A-\AP_B)_{\Jclean}$,
the same paired construction as the main estimand. This decomposition is
interpretive: it assumes an additive, parallel-shift model in which a
calibration-induced level shift on clean inputs transfers comparably to
corrupted inputs. Under an interaction model (where the intervention also
changes slope across conditions), $\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}$ mixes that
interaction with any clean-level effect; it is not a causal identification
theorem. We therefore report the J95-clean and held-out levels alongside it
rather than relying on the composite alone.

\section{Evidence layers}

Diagnostic arms (the RetinaNet decomposition: head-only and
all-except-region variants) are single-point recipe sweeps produced during
development and carry no intervals. Frozen paired evidence is the
14-condition paired protocol; its freeze covers the estimands, condition
schedule, and bootstrap schedule (the nine-block Holm adjustment lives in
the analysis code, not the frozen configuration), while the YOLO11
engines and evaluation partitions predate it and are shared with the
historical layer. Intervention evidence is the corrupt-calibration
experiment together with its codec-matched controls: the two
corruption-calibration folds were frozen prospectively before their engines
were built, while the fold-1 codec-control arms are post-hoc. Historical exploratory
results (a 144-cell grid over four datasets including TT100K, computed on
the same engines and estimand under a wider scope and a different analysis
plan) are reported
only as context and are never pooled with the frozen paired estimates.
Post-hoc extensions are the operand-level factorial inside the RetinaNet
regression head (W-only and A-only rewires of the frozen matched graph) and
the FCOS-R50-FPN replication: both were designed and executed after the
frozen paired results were known, are evaluated on the same condition
schedule with the same paired bootstrap estimand, and are reported as
post-hoc extensions rather than frozen evidence. The FCOS phase package
ships its own provenance (per-engine build manifests, quantized-ONNX
manifests, per-run training manifests, and the decoder-validation record
above) under \texttt{phase\_h\_fcos\_results/}. The pretrained-checkpoint
replication (torchvision RetinaNet-R50-FPN-v2 and FCOS-R50-FPN on a paired
2,000-image COCO val2017 subset) is likewise post hoc: its package ships
quantized-ONNX manifests, TensorRT build manifests, per-cell input/prediction/metric
records, and bootstrap draw caches under
\texttt{phase\_i\_coco\_pretrained\_results/}; calibration used the
hash-bound 512-image COCO train2017 manifest

Manuscript tables and figures are generated deterministically from frozen
ledgers by \texttt{analysis/build\_nn\_}\allowbreak\texttt{tables.py}. Engine registries,

schedule), input manifests, prediction manifests, metric records, and paired-bootstrap
draw caches are SHA-256 hash-bound and retained in the accompanying

as release v3.0.1 (version DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23074353}) under the

benchmark datasets, trained checkpoints, built TensorRT engines, and raw

transparency: corrupted, codec-control, and YOLO11 original-clean cells bind

therefore do not carry an execution-time byte-level binding for the clean

\texttt{nn\_latency\_v1\_20260930/} and

\section{Head-region activation statistics}
\label{sec:actstats}

To probe the operand-factorial attribution at the distribution level we
captured the activation tensors consumed by each RetinaNet head on the
frozen 512-image clean-calibration manifests, running the unquantized ONNX
graph in ONNX-Runtime (CUDA provider) with every head-consumed tensor
exposed as a graph output. Per-channel $|x|_{\max}$ and excess kurtosis are
accumulated by streaming raw moments over every pixel of every captured
tensor; each tensor is assigned to a region by graph consumption (tensors
feeding both heads are ``shared head inputs''). Captures cover the KITTI
and VOC trained graphs and the COCO pretrained RetinaNet graph
(\texttt{nn\_actstats/} in the evidence package; input bytes hash-verified
against the calibration manifests).

Table~\ref{tab:nn-actstats} reports the summaries. On all three graphs the
regression head is \emph{not} uniformly larger in typical magnitude---its
median channel amplitude is comparable to the classification head's---but
it is markedly heavier-tailed (on the KITTI graph, median excess kurtosis
\nnActRegKurtMedKitti{} versus \nnActClsKurtMedKitti{} for the
classification head, with per-channel maxima reaching
\nnActRegKurtMaxKitti{} versus \nnActClsKurtMaxKitti; on VOC,
\nnActRegKurtMedVoc{} versus \nnActClsKurtMedVoc; on the COCO pretrained
graph, \nnActRegKurtMedCoco{} versus \nnActClsKurtMedCoco). The tail is
broad
rather than a single stray channel: the median of the per-tensor
channel-p95 excess kurtosis is \nnActRegKurtTailKitti{} versus
\nnActClsKurtTailKitti{} on KITTI and \nnActRegKurtTailVoc{} versus
\nnActClsKurtTailVoc{} on VOC. Rare large
activations inflate a single per-tensor scale and spend the INT8 code
budget on outlier coverage, which is exactly the signature under which
activation---not weight---quantization dominates the deficit
(the operand factorial of Section~3.7 of the main text). Two cautions bound the
reading: these are activations of the fp32 ONNX graph on calibration
inputs, not the deployed quantizer's scale values (the calibrator's chosen
activation amaxes on the matched INT8 graphs are recorded alongside, e.g.\
a median of \nnActStaticRegMedKitti{} versus \nnActStaticClsMedKitti{} on
KITTI); and the captures are
a measurement of the calibration distribution, which is itself what the
quantizer saw---the tails under corrupted inputs are not measured here.

\section{Max-calibration counterfactual}
\label{sec:maxcalib}

Section~S13 shows the regression-head activations are heavy-tailed under the
entropy calibrator's inputs, which admits two distinct cost accounts: tail
clipping (the chosen range excludes outliers) or bin resolution (the range is
fine but 127 levels spread too coarsely across it). A max-calibration arm
separates the two: it sets each activation scale to the absolute
maximum observed on the calibration set, removing calibration-set clipping
at the price of the coarsest bin resolution (evaluation-time activations
beyond that maximum can still clip). We therefore ran a post-hoc counterfactual pair---INT8-matched and
INT8-selective under the max estimator, same graphs, same frozen 512-image
clean-calibration manifests, same 14-condition evaluation schedule---on the
KITTI and VOC RetinaNet detectors (\texttt{nn\_maxcalib\_v1\_20260930};
single engine builds, point estimates only, no bootstrap draws).

Table~\ref{tab:nn-maxcalib} reports the levels. The counterfactual is sharp
and negative in both directions. On KITTI, max calibration does not repair
the matched deficit---it deepens it, from \nnArmInCleanKitti{} to
\nnMcMaxJKitti{}~AP on the clean basis---while the head-exclusion benefit
persists under the new estimator (selective minus matched
\nnMcSelGainJKitti{}~AP clean, \nnMcSelGainCKitti{}~AP corrupt-mean), so the
regression-head sensitivity is not an artifact of the entropy estimator. On
VOC the max estimator is catastrophic on \emph{both} arms
($\Jclean$ clean $\approx 0.001$~AP): the detection-score distribution
collapses (the 99th-percentile detection score on the clean run drops from
$\approx 0.91$ under entropy calibration to $\approx 0.15$;
\texttt{voc\_score\_audit.json} in the evidence package), consistent with a
failure in the score (classification) pathway that regression-head
exclusion cannot rescue. A 128-image draw of the same estimator
in the earlier recipe sweep scored $\nnDiagMaxJ$~AP on the KITTI clean
basis---the max estimator's outcome is itself draw-sensitive, since a single
unlucky outlier image inflates every range.

The reading we take is bounded: clipping of the observed calibration range
is not the operative cost, because widening the range to the calibration
maximum does not recover accuracy and can destroy the classifier outright. That leaves the resolution side of
the account---a fixed 127-level grid over a wide range---as the better
candidate, though we did not separate resolution from the other numerical
changes a coarser scale induces (larger rounding bias on in-range values,
dequantization shift). Corruption-aware calibration and max calibration
together bracket the range-choice dimension: neither repairs the deficit, and
the regression-head benefit survives both.

\section{Minimum reporting checklist for paired corruption studies}

\begin{itemize}
  \item record the complete treatment recipe (checkpoint, quantizer coverage,
        calibration list and preprocessing, builder settings, decoder), not
        just the datatype label;
  \item pair treatments on identical image bytes, including terminal encoding;
  \item subtract the matched clean gap before reading a corruption
        interaction;
  \item report absolute corrupted AP and the clean gap alongside every
        interaction, so floor effects cannot masquerade as robustness;
  \item keep calibration corruption families disjoint from held-out
        evaluation families when claiming calibration-based robustness;
  \item carry codec-matched calibration controls before attributing
        calibration-intervention gains to corruption coverage;
  \item flag severity-wall cells where every treatment including FP32 is at
        the floor, and report a leave-one-out sensitivity;
  \item audit engine-build determinism before treating a serialized engine as
        a fixed artifact---tactic autotuning makes rebuilds bit-distinct, so
        hash the engine and inspector and spot-check metric spread;
  \item gate latency claims on an idle-device harness with repetitions and
        dispersion, and scope them to the measured host.
\end{itemize}
