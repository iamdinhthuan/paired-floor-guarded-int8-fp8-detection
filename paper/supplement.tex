\section{Purpose and scope}

This supplement documents the frozen protocol details, estimands, corruption
parameters, calibration schedules, and artifact contract supporting the main
article. All reported values are generated deterministically from retained
ledgers. Section~S9 additionally reports per-family $\Delta E$ diagnostics that
support, but do not extend, the main-text claims.

\section{Estimands}

For dataset $d$, model $m$, corruption condition $c$, and a pair of treatments
(A, B) sharing the same evaluator:

\begin{align}
G_0 &= \AP_A(\Jclean) - \AP_B(\Jclean), \\
G_{\mathrm{corr}} &= \overline{\AP_A(c) - \AP_B(c)} \quad\text{(equal weight over the 12 cells)}, \\
\Delta E &= G_{\mathrm{corr}} - G_0 .
\end{align}

$\Jclean$ denotes the codec-matched clean control: the $\Jclean$ clean control
and every corrupted input are terminally encoded as JPEG quality 95 with
chroma subsampling disabled, so the clean control and the corrupted inputs
differ only in the named corruption transform, not in container encoding; the
separate original-clean cell keeps the dataset-native bytes; it checks
control fidelity and anchors the clean-accuracy comparisons behind the
RetinaNet collapse and recovery claims, while the paired interaction uses
the $\Jclean$ basis. $\Delta E > 0$ means the A-minus-B gap widens under
corruption; $\Delta E < 0$ means it narrows. A narrowing gap near a shared
accuracy floor is not read as robustness: absolute corrupted AP is reported
alongside every interaction (main text, \S4.1--\S4.3).

\section{Frozen condition schedule}

Each dataset$\times$model block evaluates 14 conditions on identical image
ordering across arms (13 for the original RetinaNet FP32, FP8-legacy, and
INT8-legacy arms, which predate codec control):

\begin{itemize}
  \item clean (original bytes) and $\Jclean$ (JPEG-95 clean control);
  \item Gaussian noise: $\sigma \in \{8, 20, 40\}$ on the 0--255 RGB scale;
  \item motion blur: kernel lengths $\{5, 13, 23\}$ pixels;
  \item synthetic fog: (strength, blur radius in pixels) $\in \{(0.18,6), (0.38,12), (0.62,20)\}$;
  \item JPEG compression: qualities $\{70, 40, 15\}$.
\end{itemize}

{\sloppy Corruption parameters and the JPEG-95 terminal encoding are frozen in
versioned configuration \texttt{corruptions.json}, whose SHA-256 is recorded
as \texttt{generator\_config\_sha256} in the corruption-aware and codec-control
calibration manifests; the
frozen protocol file \texttt{nn\_paired\_protocol\_v1\_20260924} fixes the
condition list and evaluation contract on top of that generator. The same
transform code produces both the evaluation caches and the
corruption-aware calibration images.\par}

\section{Inference and evaluation contract}

Preprocessing differs by family. YOLO11 uses OpenCV bilinear resizing to a
640-pixel letterbox with centered padding of value 114 and RGB values divided
by 255. RetinaNet and FCOS divide RGB values by 255, normalize with ImageNet
means (0.485, 0.456, 0.406) and standard deviations (0.229, 0.224, 0.225),
resize bilinearly to a longest side of 640 pixels, and zero-pad the bottom
and right in normalized coordinates.
YOLO11 blocks: confidence threshold 0.001,
class-wise NMS at IoU 0.7, at most 300 detections. RetinaNet blocks:
confidence threshold 0.05, at most 1,000 pre-NMS candidates per feature level,
batched NMS at IoU 0.5, at most 300 detections. FCOS blocks: confidence threshold
0.05 on the centerness-weighted score, at most 1,000 pre-NMS candidates per
feature level, batched NMS at IoU 0.5, at most 300 detections. These are
the RetinaNet constants, reused post hoc and frozen for FCOS, not
torchvision's FCOS defaults
(score threshold 0.2, NMS IoU 0.6, 100 detections per image); the decoder is
a reimplementation of torchvision's post-processing, validated detection-for-detection
against torchvision's own pipeline on 32 seeded synthetic head-output
draws
(\texttt{phase\_h\_fcos\_results/decoder\_validation.json}: 9,600
detections, maximum box and score deviation exactly zero). The check
saturates the 300-detection cap on every draw, uses a square 640-pixel
input at scale 1.0, and covers the 8-class KITTI head only. It validates
the post-processing arithmetic, not letterbox un-mapping, sparse-output
behavior, or end-to-end engine parity. All evaluations use the COCO protocol via
\texttt{pycocotools} with \texttt{maxDets=100}; VOC and KITTI annotations were
converted once from Ultralytics YOLO labels to COCO format and frozen; VOC objects marked difficult are omitted rather than kept as ignore regions. Decoder constants are listed per family in
the paragraph above (identical for RetinaNet and FCOS) and held fixed
within each; cross-family comparisons are
comparisons of recipe bundles, never of formats alone.

\section{Corruption-aware calibration schedule}

The intervention calibration sets reuse the same 512 images as the matched
clean-calibration manifests. Each image is deterministically assigned, via a
SHA-256-keyed hash of its identifier, to exactly one of six in-family cells,
Gaussian noise or JPEG at severities 1, 3, or 5 (approximately 77--102 images
per cell). Assigned corruptions use the frozen transform parameters above, and
the outputs are terminally encoded at JPEG quality 95, matching the evaluation
input contract. In this first fold fog and motion blur never appear in
calibration and form the held-out split. A complementary second fold (schedule
\texttt{nn-corruptcalib-fold2-v1}, frozen before any of its runs) assigns the
same images to fog or motion blur at severities 1, 3, or 5 (75--95 images per
cell) and holds out Gaussian noise and JPEG. Its pre-specified rules are
(i) a held-out change from INT8-matched whose interval excludes zero
(either direction counts; the main text reads it through the directional
fold-1 criterion) and
(ii) a positive held-out $\Delta E$ with an interval excluding zero, against
both INT8-matched and INT8-q95calib.

The codec-matched control manifests encode the same 512 images at JPEG
quality 95 with no named corruption, isolating codec adaptation from
corruption coverage.

\section{Per-arm intervention contrasts}
\label{sec:supp-intervention}

The additive $\Delta E$ decomposition is itself scale-sensitive (equal
proportional changes can produce a nonzero additive interaction), so a
retention-scaled check is reported: each fold's clean-control change is scaled by
the matched arm's AP retention on that fold's held-out families
(\nnRetHoFmKitti{} for fold~1 and \nnRetHoNjKitti{} for fold~2 on KITTI;
\nnRetHoFmVoc{} and \nnRetHoNjVoc{} on VOC), predicting held-out changes of
\nnPredCcHoKitti{} and \nnPredCtHoKitti{} on KITTI and \nnPredCcHoVoc{} and
\nnPredCtHoVoc{} on VOC against the observed \nnCcHKittiP, \nnCtHKittiP,
\nnCcHVocP, and \nnCtHVocP. Under this reading both KITTI cells are largely
scale effects, VOC fold~1 still shows no corruption-specific benefit, and
only VOC fold~2 exceeds its prediction materially (\nnCtHVocP{} vs.\
\nnPredCtHoVoc{}); anchoring on the codec arm gives the same qualitative
answer (KITTI fold-1 residual \nnResidCcQHoKitti{} AP).

its clean-calibrated counterpart. This contrast (sel-corruptcalib
minus sel) is \nnSccHKitti{}

\section{Paired bootstrap}

Within each dataset$\times$model block, one deterministic schedule of
$B=2{,}000$ resamples of image indices is shared across all arms and all
conditions, so every contrast is evaluated inside common-image samples.
Resamples index evaluated images by position, so a duplicated image
contributes its detections and ground truth once per occurrence; this
multiplicity is verified by a dedicated unit test
(\texttt{tests/test\_bootstrap\_multiplicity.py}), which constructs a
stub evaluation where resample $[0,0,1]$ scores $34/101$ against the
$51/101$ that deduplication would give. Reported point estimates are
full-sample plug-in values; reported intervals are the 2.5th--97.5th
percentiles of the bootstrap distribution of each contrast. Intervals cover
image resampling only; they do not cover training-seed, calibration-draw,
engine-build, or corruption-realization variance, and image-level resampling
treats frames as independent (KITTI frames from shared driving sequences are
correlated, so those intervals may be optimistic).

Two-sided bootstrap $p$-values use a consistent plus-one convention in each
tail, $\widetilde p=\min\{1,\,2(b_{\min}+1)/(B+1)\}$, so the smallest
attainable value at $B=2{,}000$ is $2/2001\approx0.001$ and no $p$ is ever
exactly zero; Holm adjustment covers the nine paired YOLO11 blocks. Heterogeneity of
$\Delta E$ across those blocks uses Cochran's
$Q=\sum_i w_i(\hat\Delta_i-\bar\Delta_w)^2$ with $w_i=1/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_i^2$
from the bootstrap draws, $I^2=\max\{0,(Q-\mathrm{df})/Q\}$, and the
DerSimonian--Laird between-block standard deviation $\tau$. Blocks within a
dataset share images, so their estimates may be correlated; the cross-block
covariance is not identifiable from the phase-B caches because each block's
resample schedule is seeded per dataset$\times$model. To bound the induced
dependence, the nine YOLO11 blocks were rerun with $B=1{,}000$ under a
schedule seeded per dataset only (\texttt{shared\_schedule/bootstrap/}):
per-draw Pearson correlations of the FP8$-$INT8 $\Delta E$ between model
scales are $r=\{0.240, 0.213, -0.104\}$ on KITTI (n$\sim$m, n$\sim$x,
m$\sim$x), $\{-0.033, -0.066, 0.111\}$ on VOC, and $\{0.057, 0.013, -0.196\}$
on the COCO subset. These are small and of mixed sign. Using this rerun's full within-dataset covariance
matrix (blocks on different datasets share no images and are treated as
independent; point estimates are the frozen plug-in values), the generalized
Cochran's $Q=\mathbf e^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$, with $\mathbf e=\hat{\boldsymbol\Delta}-\hat\mu_{\mathrm{GLS}}\mathbf 1$ and
$\hat\mu_{\mathrm{GLS}}=(\mathbf 1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol\Delta})/(\mathbf 1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1)$, is
$\nnHetGlsQ$ on $\nnHetDf$ df ($p\approx\nnHetGlsP$), against $\nnHetQ$
under independence; within-dataset values are $\nnHetGlsQKitti$ (KITTI,
$p=\nnHetGlsPKitti$), $\nnHetGlsQVoc$ (VOC, $p\approx\nnHetGlsPVoc$), and
$\nnHetGlsQCoco$ (COCO, $p\approx\nnHetGlsPCoco$) on 2 df, and the VOC
YOLO11n$-$YOLO11m contrast has $z=\nnVocNmZGls$ with its covariance included
(\texttt{analysis/nn\_final\_stats.py}, \texttt{gls\_heterogeneity}). The
dependence therefore does not change the heterogeneity conclusion, and it does
not affect the per-block Holm adjustment, which requires valid marginal
$p$-values but no independence among tests. RetinaNet and
intervention intervals are per contrast.

As a cross-machine check, the pretrained-FCOS block was bootstrapped
independently on two hosts under the same seed namespace
(\texttt{coco\_\_fcos\_pretrained}): the 56 cell-level draw arrays agree to
$2.2\times10^{-16}$ absolute difference (identical seed, $B$, and image
set), so resample schedules are portable; container file hashes still
differ because NPZ serialization embeds metadata.

Point estimates are full-sample plug-in values computed from the metric
records of each cell (for COCO, from the frozen 2,000-image subset
evaluation); bootstrap draws supply intervals, standard errors, and
$p$-values only. For the intervention, the corruption-specific component on
the held-out family is
$\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}=(\AP_A-\AP_B)_{\text{held-out}}-(\AP_A-\AP_B)_{\Jclean}$,
the same paired construction as the main estimand. It is an arithmetic
contrast on the AP scale: an equal additive intervention effect on clean and
held-out inputs cancels exactly. Reading a nonzero value as a corruption-specific
benefit requires the clean-level effect to transfer additively; scale and floor
effects can otherwise produce a nonzero interaction, and the contrast does not
identify a causal mechanism. The J95-clean and held-out levels are therefore
reported alongside it.

\section{Sensitivity analyses}

Table~\ref{tab:supp-perfamily} resolves the paired $\Delta E$ of each
YOLO11 block per corruption family (each entry is the family-mean FP8$-$INT8
gap minus the $\Jclean$ gap) and gives the leave-one-out exclusion of the
\texttt{motion\_blur-s5} severity wall. The sign heterogeneity is also
\emph{within} blocks: on VOC-x the JPEG family contributes $\nnFamVocXJpeg$~AP while
Gaussian noise and motion blur contribute $\nnFamVocXGn$ and $\nnFamVocXMb$~AP;
on COCO-m JPEG ($\nnFamCocoMJpeg$) and
motion blur ($\nnFamCocoMMb$) contribute positively while noise contributes a
null negative ($\nnFamCocoMGn$).
Motion blur is the most block-variable family of the four (across-block
standard deviation $\approx$\nnFamSDpMb{} AP population, $\approx$\nnFamSDsMb{} sample), with
JPEG close behind ($\approx$\nnFamSDpJpeg); neither is uniformly
harmful or helpful. Excluding the severity wall leaves the
cross-block heterogeneity essentially unchanged ($Q\approx\nnHetQEx$,
$I^2\approx\nnHetIsqEx\%$) and every Holm-surviving block's sign intact; the two
KITTI blocks that do not survive Holm drift (YOLO11n's null point estimate flips from
$\nnDEKittiN$ to $\nnDEexKittiN$, and YOLO11m's unadjusted-significant negative turns
null). The interaction
pattern is therefore not an artifact of the saturated cell, although the
marginal KITTI estimates are sensitive to it.

The same leave-one-out sensitivity applied to the intervention contrasts
changes no frozen-criterion verdict; two secondary codec-control contrasts do
move across the zero boundary on VOC (both on the held-out basis):
INT8-corruptcalib minus INT8-q95calib goes from \nnCcQHVoc{} to
\nnCcQHxVoc{}, and INT8-sel-q95calib minus INT8-selective goes from
\nnSqqHVoc{} to \nnSqqHxVoc{}. Both are read with the
main text's no-multiplicity caveat.

\section{Evidence layers}

Diagnostic arms (the RetinaNet decomposition: head-only and
all-except-region variants) are single-point recipe sweeps produced during
development and carry no intervals. Frozen paired evidence is the
14-condition paired protocol; its freeze covers the estimands, condition

the analysis code, not the frozen configuration), while the YOLO11

historical layer. Intervention evidence is the corrupt-calibration
experiment together with its codec-matched controls: the two
corruption-calibration folds were frozen prospectively before their engines

results (a 144-cell grid over four datasets including TT100K, computed on

frozen paired results were known, are evaluated on the same condition

\section{Head-region activation statistics}
\label{sec:actstats}

To probe the operand-factorial attribution at the distribution level, the activation tensors consumed by each RetinaNet and FCOS head were captured on
the frozen 512-image clean-calibration manifests by running the unquantized ONNX
graph in ONNX-Runtime (CUDA provider) with every head-consumed tensor
exposed as a graph output. Per-channel $|x|_{\max}$ and excess kurtosis are
computed over all spatial positions in the 512 captured images, with moments
accumulated by streaming over every pixel. Each tensor's per-channel
statistics are first summarized by their median, then the regional and
per-level entries report medians across tensors. Each tensor is assigned to
a region by graph consumption (tensors
feeding both heads are ``shared head inputs''). Captures cover the KITTI
and VOC trained RetinaNet and FCOS graphs and the COCO pretrained RetinaNet graph
(\texttt{nn\_actstats/} in the evidence package; input bytes hash-verified
against the calibration manifests).

Table~\ref{tab:nn-actstats} reports the summaries. On the three RetinaNet
graphs the regression and classification heads have comparable median channel
amplitudes, but regression-head activations have much heavier tails (on the KITTI graph, median excess kurtosis
\nnActRegKurtMedKitti{} versus \nnActClsKurtMedKitti{} for the
classification head, with per-channel maxima reaching
\nnActRegKurtMaxKitti{} versus \nnActClsKurtMaxKitti; on VOC,
\nnActRegKurtMedVoc{} versus \nnActClsKurtMedVoc; on the COCO pretrained
graph, \nnActRegKurtMedCoco{} versus \nnActClsKurtMedCoco). The tail is
broad
rather than a single stray channel: the median of the per-tensor
channel-p95 excess kurtosis is \nnActRegKurtTailKitti{} versus
\nnActClsKurtTailKitti{} on KITTI and \nnActRegKurtTailVoc{} versus
\nnActClsKurtTailVoc{} on VOC. Rare large
activations inflate a single per-tensor scale and spend the INT8 code
budget on outlier coverage, which is exactly the signature under which
activation quantization, not weight quantization, dominates the deficit
(the operand factorial of Section~3.7 of the main text). Two cautions bound the
reading: these are activations of the fp32 ONNX graph on calibration
inputs, not the deployed quantizer's scale values (the calibrator's chosen
activation amaxes on the matched INT8 graphs are recorded alongside, e.g.\
a median of \nnActStaticRegMedKitti{} versus \nnActStaticClsMedKitti{} on
KITTI); and the captures are
a measurement of the calibration distribution, which is itself what the
quantizer saw. Tails under corrupted inputs are not measured here.

Table~\ref{tab:nn-actstats-level} resolves the same captures per FPN level,
and a structural audit of the matched QDQ graphs
(\texttt{nn\_actstats/qdq\_structure\_*.json}) records how the head's
activation ranges are parametrized. Two structure-level facts matter. First,
the regression head does not share one activation scale across pyramid
levels: the matched graph carries 25 regression-head-consumed activation
quantizers (the five FPN feature tensors at the head input, shared
with the classification head, plus four tower tensors at each of the five FPN levels), and every one is per-tensor with a
level-specific scale, so a shared head-wide activation range is not the
mechanism here. Second, the tail burden is level-ordered: regression-head
median excess kurtosis is highest at the fine levels that carry
small-object predictions (\nnActRegKurtPThreeKitti{} at P3 on KITTI,
falling to \nnActRegKurtPSevenKitti{} at P7; the classification head shows a
much flatter profile, \nnActClsKurtPThreeKitti{} to
\nnActClsKurtPSevenKitti), and the same ordering holds on VOC; on the pretrained COCO graph the peak is
at P4 (\nnActRegKurtPFourCoco{} versus \nnActRegKurtPThreeCoco{} at P3). Per-tensor grids at P3/P4 therefore spend the most
code budget covering outliers. This fits the matched arm's
deficit, which is concentrated in AP$_S$ and AP$_{75}$ (Table~11 of the
main text), but it is a consistency argument, not a causal test.

The FCOS captures provide a cross-architecture falsification check for the
extreme-tail profile, not evidence that FCOS activations are tail-free.
Regression-head median excess kurtosis at P3/P4 is
\nnActRegKurtPThreeFcosKitti{}/\nnActRegKurtPFourFcosKitti{} on KITTI and
\nnActRegKurtPThreeFcosVoc{}/\nnActRegKurtPFourFcosVoc{} on VOC, compared
with \nnActRegKurtPThreeKitti{}/\nnActRegKurtPFourKitti{} and
\nnActRegKurtPThreeVoc{}/\nnActRegKurtPFourVoc{} for RetinaNet. In
particular, FCOS still has positive excess kurtosis at KITTI P3; its fine-level
tails are substantially lighter, not absent. The combination of lighter
fine-level tails and no comparable head-specific deficit is consistent
with the proposed distribution-level account, but FCOS also differs in
architecture, anchor density, centerness scoring, and selective-output
coverage, so the comparison does not isolate tail shape causally.

\section{Max-calibration counterfactual}
\label{sec:maxcalib}

Section~S13 shows the regression-head activations are heavy-tailed under the
entropy calibrator's inputs, which admits two distinct cost accounts: tail
clipping (the chosen range excludes outliers) or bin resolution (the range is
fine but wide relative to the signed INT8 grid spacing). A max-calibration arm
changes clipping and resolution jointly: it sets each activation range bound to the absolute
maximum observed on the calibration set, removing calibration-set clipping
at the price of the coarsest bin resolution (evaluation-time activations
beyond that maximum can still clip). A post-hoc counterfactual pair was therefore run, INT8-matched and
INT8-selective under the max estimator with the same graphs, frozen 512-image
clean-calibration manifests, and 14-condition evaluation schedule, on the
KITTI and VOC RetinaNet detectors (\texttt{nn\_maxcalib\_v1\_20260930};
single engine builds, point estimates only, no bootstrap draws).

Table~\ref{tab:nn-maxcalib} reports the levels. The counterfactual is sharp
and negative in both directions. On KITTI, max calibration does not repair
the matched deficit but deepens it, from \nnArmInCleanKitti{} to
\nnMcMaxJKitti{}~AP on the clean basis, while the head-exclusion benefit
persists under the new estimator (selective minus matched
\nnMcSelGainJKitti{}~AP clean, \nnMcSelGainCKitti{}~AP corrupt-mean), so the
regression-head sensitivity is not an artifact of the entropy estimator. On
VOC the max estimator is catastrophic on \emph{both} arms
(Table~\ref{tab:nn-maxcalib}; clean AP $\approx0.001$--$0.002$): the detection-score distribution
collapses (the 99th-percentile detection score on the clean run drops from
$\approx 0.91$ under entropy calibration to $\approx 0.15$;
\texttt{voc\_score\_audit.json} in the evidence package), consistent with a
failure in the score (classification) pathway that regression-head
exclusion cannot rescue. A 128-image draw of the same estimator
in the earlier recipe sweep scored $\nnDiagMaxJ$~AP on the KITTI clean
basis. The two calibration budgets give different AP values; variability across
repeated calibration draws at a fixed budget was not measured.

Because the graph-wide swap perturbs every quantizer, it cannot separate a
head-local estimator effect from collateral damage elsewhere (the VOC
collapse is the extreme case). A second counterfactual was therefore run,
restricted to the regression head's inputs (\texttt{nn\_maxreg\_v1\_20261001};
same single-build, point-estimate-only protocol): starting from the frozen
INT8-matched graphs, the entropy-calibrated scales of every activation
quantizer consumed by a regression-head node (25 per graph, per-tensor: 20
inside the tower, one per conv per FPN level, plus the five FPN-boundary
quantizers at the head input; Section~S13) were replaced by the
calibration-set maxima measured on the same captured activations, while
every other quantizer (backbone and classification tower) kept its
entropy-calibrated scale byte-identical. The five boundary quantizers are
shared with the classification head, so the patch also changes the
classification head's input scales; it is regression-head-local inside the
tower but not at the shared boundary. The patch is implemented by \texttt{src/patch\_maxreg\_scales.py}: each
selected quantizer's Q and DQ scale becomes
$\mathrm{fp16}(|x|_{\max}/127)$, with $|x|_{\max}$ the largest per-channel
absolute maximum captured for that tensor (Section~S13). Re-running the script
on the frozen matched graphs reproduces the archived patched graphs exactly:
byte-identical on KITTI, and identical nodes and initializers on VOC (the file
hash differs only through serialization;
\texttt{phase\_k\_maxreg\_results/patch\_reproduction.json}). This arm leaves VOC functional and still does not repair
the deficit (Table~\ref{tab:nn-maxcalib}): original-clean AP sits at
\nnMcMaxRegJKitti{}~AP on KITTI and \nnMcMaxRegJVoc{}~AP on VOC, against
\nnArmInCleanKitti{}/\nnArmInCleanVoc{} for the entropy-matched arms, and the
corrupt-mean levels (\nnMcMaxRegCKitti{}/\nnMcMaxRegCVoc{}~AP) track the
entropy-matched arms within about one AP point. Widening the
regression-head-consumed ranges to the observed maxima recovers nothing.

The interpretation is bounded. Extending ranges to the observed
calibration maxima, graph-wide or restricted to regression-head-consumed
quantizers, does not improve AP and can destroy the classifier outright.
Because max calibration reduces calibration-set clipping and coarsens the
grid at the same time, these experiments do not separate a clipping benefit
from the accompanying resolution cost; they are compatible with a
resolution account (a fixed signed INT8 grid over a wide range) but do not
establish it. Resolution is also not separated from the other numerical
changes a coarser scale induces (larger rounding bias on in-range values,
dequantization shift), and max calibration does not preclude
evaluation-time clipping of activations beyond the calibration-set maximum.
Corruption-aware calibration and max calibration together bracket the
range-choice dimension: neither repairs the deficit, and the
regression-head benefit survives both.

\section{Minimum reporting checklist for paired corruption studies}

\begin{itemize}
  \item record the complete treatment recipe (checkpoint, quantizer coverage,
        calibration list and preprocessing, builder settings, decoder), not
        just the datatype label;
  \item pair treatments on identical image bytes, including terminal encoding;
  \item subtract the matched clean gap before reading a corruption
        interaction;
  \item report absolute corrupted AP and the clean gap alongside every
        interaction, so floor effects cannot masquerade as robustness;
  \item keep calibration corruption families disjoint from held-out
        evaluation families when claiming calibration-based robustness;
  \item carry codec-matched calibration controls before attributing
        calibration-intervention gains to corruption coverage;
  \item flag severity-wall cells where every treatment including FP32 is at
        the floor, and report a leave-one-out sensitivity;
  \item audit engine-build determinism before treating a serialized engine as
        a fixed artifact; tactic autotuning makes rebuilds bit-distinct, so
        hash the engine and inspector and spot-check metric spread;
  \item gate latency claims on an idle-device harness with repetitions and
        dispersion, and scope them to the measured host.
\end{itemize}
