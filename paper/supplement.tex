\section{Purpose and scope}

This supplement documents the frozen protocol details, estimands, corruption
parameters, calibration schedules, and artifact contract supporting the main
article. All reported values are generated deterministically from retained
ledgers. Section~S8 additionally reports per-family $\Delta E$ diagnostics that
support---but do not extend---the main-text claims.

\section{Estimands}

For dataset $d$, model $m$, corruption condition $c$, and a pair of treatments
(A, B) sharing the same evaluator:

\begin{align}
G_0 &= \AP_A(\Jclean) - \AP_B(\Jclean), \\
G_c &= \overline{\AP_A(c) - \AP_B(c)} \quad\text{(equal weight over the 12 cells)}, \\
\Delta E &= G_c - G_0 .
\end{align}

$\Jclean$ denotes the codec-matched clean control: the $\Jclean$ clean control
and every corrupted input are terminally encoded as JPEG quality 95 with
chroma subsampling disabled, so the clean control and the corrupted inputs
differ only in the named corruption transform, not in container encoding; the
separate original-clean cell keeps the dataset-native bytes; it checks
control fidelity and anchors the clean-accuracy comparisons behind the
RetinaNet collapse and recovery claims, while the paired interaction uses
the $\Jclean$ basis. $\Delta E > 0$ means the A-minus-B gap widens under
corruption; $\Delta E < 0$ means it narrows. A narrowing gap near a shared
accuracy floor is not read as robustness: absolute corrupted AP is reported
alongside every interaction (main text, \S4.1--\S4.3).

\section{Frozen condition schedule}

Each dataset$\times$model block evaluates 14 conditions on identical image
ordering for quantized arms (13 for the RetinaNet FP32 and INT8-legacy arms
that predate codec control):

\begin{itemize}
  \item clean (original bytes) and $\Jclean$ (JPEG-95 clean control);
  \item Gaussian noise: $\sigma \in \{8, 20, 40\}$;
  \item motion blur: kernel lengths $\{5, 13, 23\}$;
  \item synthetic fog: (strength, radius) $\in \{(0.18,6), (0.38,12), (0.62,20)\}$;
  \item JPEG compression: qualities $\{70, 40, 15\}$.
\end{itemize}

{\sloppy Corruption parameters and the JPEG-95 terminal encoding are frozen in
versioned configuration \texttt{corruptions.json}, whose SHA-256 is recorded
as \texttt{generator\_config\_sha256} in the corruption-aware and codec-control
calibration manifests; the
frozen protocol file \texttt{nn\_paired\_protocol\_v1\_20260924} fixes the
condition list and evaluation contract on top of that generator. The same
transform code produces both the evaluation caches and the
corruption-aware calibration images.\par}

All INT8/FP8 engines are built by TensorRT 11.1.0.106 from ONNX graphs
quantized by NVIDIA ModelOpt 0.45.0 (entropy calibration, 512-image budget;
the diagnostic sweeps vary the estimator and include arms marked
``128-img'' in Table~4 of the main text, which use the earlier 128-image

with a 4,096~MiB builder workspace. Builder policy is recorded per engine
and differs by program: the YOLO11 engines were built by \texttt{trtexec}
before the frozen contract and carry TensorRT~11.1 defaults (TF32 enabled),
while every RetinaNet arm built for this study passes \texttt{--noTF32}.
Calibration and evaluation images are disjoint. Checkpoints are one per
dataset$\times$model cell, and their selection provenance is disclosed: the
KITTI and VOC YOLO11n/m/x models were fine-tuned from Ultralytics pretrained
weights under the pilot program (\texttt{*\_train\_v1} runs, dataset YAMLs:
KITTI \texttt{val:\,images/val}, VOC \texttt{val:\,images/test2007}), with
the best checkpoint taken on the same

official pretrained checkpoint without a dataset fine-tune; the torchvision
RetinaNet-R50-FPN-v2 model is fine-tuned per dataset for 100 epochs at
640-pixel input (learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$,
deterministic seed 20260807), selecting the best checkpoint by minimum
validation loss on the same evaluation partition. Per-run training
manifests are hash-bound; the evaluation-partition overlap is a disclosed
selection caveat (see the main text's limitations). The frozen condition schedule is
14 conditions per arm; the RetinaNet FP32 and legacy arms predate codec
control and carry 13.

\section{Inference and evaluation contract}

YOLO11 blocks: 640-pixel letterbox input, confidence threshold 0.001,
class-wise NMS at IoU 0.7, at most 300 detections. RetinaNet blocks: 640-pixel
letterbox input, confidence threshold 0.05, batched NMS at IoU 0.5, at most
300 detections. All evaluations use the COCO protocol via
\texttt{pycocotools} with \texttt{maxDets=100}; VOC and KITTI annotations were
converted once to COCO format and frozen. Decoder constants differ across
families and are held fixed within each; cross-family comparisons are
comparisons of recipe bundles, never of formats alone.

\section{Corruption-aware calibration schedule}

The intervention calibration sets reuse the same 512 images as the matched
clean-calibration manifests. Each image is deterministically assigned---via a
SHA-256-keyed hash of its identifier---to exactly one of six in-family cells,
Gaussian noise or JPEG at severities 1, 3, or 5 (approximately 77--102 images
per cell). Assigned corruptions use the frozen transform parameters above, and
the outputs are terminally encoded at JPEG quality 95, matching the evaluation
input contract. In this first fold fog and motion blur never appear in
calibration and form the held-out split. A complementary second fold (schedule
\texttt{nn-corruptcalib-fold2-v1}, frozen before any of its runs) assigns the
same images to fog or motion blur at severities 1, 3, or 5 (75--95 images per
cell) and holds out Gaussian noise and JPEG. Its pre-specified rules are
(i) a held-out gain over INT8-matched with an interval excluding zero and
(ii) a positive held-out $\Delta E$ with an interval excluding zero, against
both INT8-matched and INT8-q95calib.

The codec-matched control manifests encode the same 512 images at JPEG
quality 95 with no named corruption, isolating codec adaptation from
corruption coverage.

\section{Paired bootstrap}

Within each dataset$\times$model block, one deterministic schedule of
$B=2{,}000$ resamples of image indices is shared across all arms and all
conditions, so every contrast is evaluated inside common-image samples.
Reported point estimates are full-sample plug-in values; reported intervals
are the 2.5th--97.5th percentiles of the bootstrap distribution of each
contrast. Intervals cover image resampling only; they do not cover
training-seed, calibration-draw, engine-build, or corruption-realization
variance, and image-level resampling treats frames as independent (KITTI
frames from shared driving sequences are correlated, so those intervals may
be optimistic).

Two-sided bootstrap $p$-values are
$p=\min\{1,2\min[\Pr(d\le 0),\Pr(d\ge 0)]\}$ (two-sided resolution $1/1000$) and are
Holm-adjusted across the nine paired YOLO11 blocks. Heterogeneity of
$\Delta E$ across those blocks uses Cochran's
$Q=\sum_i w_i(\hat\Delta_i-\bar\Delta_w)^2$ with $w_i=1/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_i^2$
from the bootstrap draws, $I^2=\max\{0,(Q-\mathrm{df})/Q\}$, and the
DerSimonian--Laird between-block standard deviation $\tau$. Blocks within a
dataset share images, so their estimates may be correlated; the cross-block
covariance is not estimated, and treating the blocks as independent is an
approximation whose error direction is not established for these
differences-of-differences (positive covariance would make $Q$ and the
pairwise $z$ statistics conservative, but this is an assumption, not a
verified property). RetinaNet and
intervention intervals are per contrast.

Point estimates are full-sample plug-in values computed from the metric
records of each cell (for COCO, from the frozen 2,000-image subset
evaluation); bootstrap draws supply intervals, standard errors, and
$p$-values only. For the intervention, the corruption-specific component on
the held-out family is
$\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}=(\AP_A-\AP_B)_{\text{held-out}}-(\AP_A-\AP_B)_{\Jclean}$,
the same paired construction as the main estimand. This decomposition is
interpretive: it assumes an additive, parallel-shift model in which a
calibration-induced level shift on clean inputs transfers comparably to
corrupted inputs. Under an interaction model (where the intervention also
changes slope across conditions), $\Delta E_{\text{held-out}}$ mixes that
interaction with any clean-level effect; it is not a causal identification
theorem. We therefore report the J95-clean and held-out levels alongside it
rather than relying on the composite alone.

\section{Sensitivity analyses}

Table~\ref{tab:supp-perfamily} resolves the paired $\Delta E$ of each
YOLO11 block per corruption family (each entry is the family-mean FP8$-$INT8
gap minus the $\Jclean$ gap) and gives the leave-one-out exclusion of the
\texttt{motion\_blur-s5} severity wall. The sign heterogeneity is also
\emph{within} blocks: on VOC-x the JPEG family contributes $-1.07$~AP while
Gaussian noise and motion blur contribute $+0.7$~AP each; on COCO-m JPEG and
motion blur contribute positively while noise contributes a null negative.
Motion blur is the most block-variable family of the four (across-block
standard deviation $\approx$1.1 AP population, $\approx$1.2 sample), with
JPEG close behind ($\approx$1.0); neither is uniformly
harmful or helpful. Excluding the severity wall leaves the
cross-block heterogeneity essentially unchanged ($Q\approx60.2$,
$I^2\approx87\%$) and every Holm-surviving block's sign intact; the two
near-null KITTI blocks drift (YOLO11n flips from $-0.01$ to $+0.15$, still
null, and YOLO11m's negative effect loses significance). The interaction
pattern is therefore not an artifact of the saturated cell, although the
marginal KITTI estimates are sensitive to it.

The same leave-one-out sensitivity applied to the intervention contrasts
changes no frozen-criterion verdict; two secondary codec-control contrasts do
move across the zero boundary on VOC (both on the held-out basis):
INT8-corruptcalib minus INT8-q95calib goes from \nnCcQHVoc{} to
\nnCcQHxVoc{}, and INT8-sel-q95calib minus INT8-selective goes from
\nnSqqHVoc{} to \nnSqqHxVoc{}. Both are read with the
main text's no-multiplicity caveat.

\section{Evidence layers}

Diagnostic arms (the RetinaNet decomposition: head-only and
all-except-region variants) are single-point recipe sweeps produced during
development and carry no intervals. Frozen paired evidence is the
14-condition paired protocol; its freeze covers the estimands, condition
schedule, and bootstrap schedule (the nine-block Holm adjustment lives in
the analysis code, not the frozen configuration), while the YOLO11
engines and evaluation partitions predate it and are shared with the
historical layer. Intervention evidence is the corrupt-calibration
experiment together with its codec-matched controls: the two
corruption-calibration folds were frozen prospectively before their engines
were built, while the fold-1 codec-control arms are post-hoc. Historical exploratory
results (a 144-cell grid over four datasets including TT100K, computed on
the same engines and estimand under a wider scope and a different analysis
plan) are reported
only as context and are never pooled with the frozen paired estimates.

\section{Artifact and audit boundary}

Manuscript tables and figures are generated deterministically from frozen
ledgers by \texttt{analysis/build\_nn\_tables.py}. Engine registries,
calibration manifests (including the corrupt-calibration cell-assignment
schedule), input manifests, prediction manifests, metric records, and paired-bootstrap
draw caches are SHA-256 hash-bound and retained in the accompanying
compact-evidence package, deposited in the versioned research archive
under the concept DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22031663}
(currently resolving to the prior CVIU-era release lineage; the Neural
Networks release mints on publication). Raw
benchmark datasets, trained checkpoints, built TensorRT engines, and raw
predictions are not redistributed; their provenance and regeneration path is
documented in the archive. One binding asymmetry is disclosed for
transparency: corrupted and codec-control cells bind each run to a
content-addressed input manifest covering the encoded bytes, whereas the
original-clean cells bind the frozen dataset split through its annotation
SHA-256 (\texttt{clean-root:<hash>}). Per-image clean manifests exist in
the archive (e.g.\ \texttt{kitti\_val\_clean\_ultralytics\_v1}) and establish
the reference bytes of each frozen split, but they are not bound into the
clean run records; the runs therefore do not carry an execution-time
byte-level binding for the clean inputs, only the annotation-level
identifier.

\section{Minimum reporting checklist for paired corruption studies}

\begin{itemize}
  \item record the complete treatment recipe (checkpoint, quantizer coverage,
        calibration list and preprocessing, builder settings, decoder), not
        just the datatype label;
  \item pair treatments on identical image bytes, including terminal encoding;
  \item subtract the matched clean gap before reading a corruption
        interaction;
  \item report absolute corrupted AP and the clean gap alongside every
        interaction, so floor effects cannot masquerade as robustness;
  \item keep calibration corruption families disjoint from held-out
        evaluation families when claiming calibration-based robustness;
  \item carry codec-matched calibration controls before attributing
        calibration-intervention gains to corruption coverage;
  \item flag severity-wall cells where every treatment including FP32 is at
        the floor, and report a leave-one-out sensitivity.
\end{itemize}
