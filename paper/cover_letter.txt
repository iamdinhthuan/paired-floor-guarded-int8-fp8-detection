Dear Editors of Neural Networks,

Please consider our manuscript, "Recipe determinants of quantization fragility under image corruption: paired evidence and a regression-head localization in object detectors," for publication as a research article in Neural Networks.

Deployment of low-precision neural networks raises a reliability question that current evaluation practice answers ambiguously: whether an 8-bit quantized detector remains reliable under corrupted inputs depends on how the engine was built, not only on the datatype label. We make this concrete with a paired, codec-controlled protocol in which every corrupted input shares bytes with a JPEG-95 clean control, and the corruption-associated change in the FP8-INT8 average-precision gap is estimated inside common-image bootstrap samples.

Three findings support the submission. First, on a frozen protocol covering YOLO11n/m/x on KITTI, VOC, and a 2,000-image COCO subset, the interaction reverses sign across dataset-by-scale cells, so no universal INT8-versus-FP8 robustness ranking exists. Second, a matched-recipe decomposition of RetinaNet localizes a severe INT8 collapse (-21.2 AP on KITTI) to regression-head quantization; excluding that head alone restores clean-competitive accuracy without retraining. Third, a controlled two-fold calibration intervention---calibrating INT8 on corruption-augmented images and testing on held-out corruption families under complementary splits, with codec-matched calibration controls---shows the intervention itself is context-dependent (transferring in one fold-by-dataset cell, significantly harming another) and and finds clean-only calibration ranges are not supported as a reliable repair for the corruption fragility. The paper therefore converts a measurement observation into a validated localization with a practical selective-precision recipe, with deployment cost explicitly left unevaluated.

The work fits Neural Networks' scope on the reliability and deployment behavior of neural network models: it treats quantization as a property of the executable recipe, identifies the fragile network region, and validates an intervention, rather than reporting a format leaderboard.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all six authors have approved its submission. The research received no specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors. Code and compact evidence are archived under concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22031663; raw datasets, checkpoints, built engines, and raw predictions are not redistributed, with their regeneration boundaries documented. The manuscript contains data-and-code, funding, competing-interest, CRediT, and generative-AI declarations.
