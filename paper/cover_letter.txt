Dear Editors of Computer Vision and Image Understanding,

Please consider our manuscript, “A Paired, Floor-Guarded Evaluation Protocol for INT8 and FP8 Object Detectors under Image Corruptions,” for publication as a research article in Computer Vision and Image Understanding.

Reliable evaluation of a deployed vision system requires separating a performance difference already present on nominal images from a change associated with visual degradation. We address this measurement problem for executable INT8 and FP8 object detectors. Our byte-matched, image-paired four-cell protocol subtracts the matched-clean treatment gap from the corrupted-input gap within common-image bootstrap samples. The resulting quantity is an algebraic interaction contrast, not a causal estimator, and is interpreted only after clean fidelity and absolute corrupted accuracy have been checked.

The exploratory grid contains 144 conditions across four datasets, three YOLO11 capacities, four synthetic corruptions, and three severities. We separately examine post-selection evaluation on final VOC/KITTI images excluded from checkpoint selection, interaction-scale and corruption-materialization sensitivities, training and calibration seeds, aggregation and class composition, matched engine runtime, and recipe-portability stress tests with RT-DETR and RetinaNet. Although the raw corrupted gap favors FP8 in 134 of 144 exploratory conditions, clean adjustment produces 56 point-estimate sign reversals; 17 adjusted intervals lie entirely below zero and 39 cross zero. FP8 outperforms INT8 by 1.40 matched-clean average-precision points on average, whereas the balanced adjusted interaction is -0.02 points in the broader grid and -0.55 points on the VOC/KITTI final holdouts. Absolute accuracy nevertheless decreases in 282 of 288 corrupted arms, demonstrating why scale-explicit interactions and remaining task performance must be reported together.

The manuscript fits the journal’s focus on recognition and complete vision systems because its primary contribution is an auditable evaluation methodology under visual degradation, rather than a vendor-specific datatype leaderboard. The protocol generalizes to comparisons of quantizers, mixed-precision policies, compilers, calibration methods, and runtimes whenever a pre-existing clean difference can be confused with a degradation-specific effect.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all six authors have approved its submission. The research received no specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors.
